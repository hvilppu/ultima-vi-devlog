The rules nothing called

Two slices this round, F51 and F53, and one story behind both: the difference between built and working. For each one a rule already existed, measured and tested, and nothing called it. One got wired by noticing it in review; the other got a single collecting entry point built for it this round. This is the most player visible pair of changes in a long while.

Provoke: a wrong sentence in five places

The measurement overturned a sentence this project had written in five places: the victim's alignment is not flipped from its own value, it is set from the attacker's own. A good attacker (0x40) makes the victim chaotic (0x60); an evil (0x20), chaotic or neutral attacker all make it good. One answer against three, and a good attacker alone cannot tell the right reading from the wrong one, which is exactly why the flipped reading survived this long unnoticed.

A scheduled victim becomes a vigilante and the routine returns before the alignment is set, so its alignment never changes; a temporary monster gets the assault behaviour after the alignment set, in the same path, not instead of it. The wrong sentence sat in two places in the plan and three in the code. The first sweep found two, the second found three more, because the plan writes the claim as one compound word and the code writes it as a noun. All five are corrected, including published devlog post 98, which was wrong when it was written.

And the build pass left the result with no caller at all. That would have made provoke the fifth thing in this project that is correct, tested, and reached by nothing; it was wired during review. This is the first time hitting a neutral townsperson does anything to the townsperson.

The minute that finally spends

The clock already advanced: one minute per granted round. But nothing spent that minute, so three rules that were measured, built and tested, the seven status rolls, the spell duration countdown, and the magic regained per hour, all had no caller. Every status a spell applied was permanent and every duration was forever, not because a rule was missing but because nothing ran it.

Now the whole world clock routine runs: the time stop that returns before everything else, the sixteen effect counters, one eruption draw on every call (including a zero minute refresh), every player controlled character once per call with its torch and three worn items, the seven status rolls once per minute, the calendar's own carry across the hour boundary with magic and level regained, and the ambient light as a number from zero to seven with dawn and dusk ramps.

What a player sees: invisibility, protection, curses, charm, paralysis, sleep and poison now end on their own, measured odds. A caster regains magic every hour. A torch burns down and three worn items can vanish on their own sentinels.

Three corrections, because they are the point of this post

One: I claimed the clock never advances, and that was wrong. The claim came from searching for one method name and finding only a startup call and a midnight jump; the clock is advanced inside the turn scheduler, and the measurement confirms the original does exactly the same, one minute per round. A conclusion from a name search instead of a call path.

Two: a fault of the original's own, tidied away, had to be put back. The "wears an invisibility ring" flag is declared once for the whole routine and cleared only in the player controlled branch, so a party member's ring suppresses the next unordered actor's own roll to become visible again. The state is reachable, so it is reproduced, not fixed. Its test's seed was found by scanning, not picked: no seed below about 2000 makes the unsuppressed roll land, which is the random generator's own documented wart that the first values after a small seed are poor.

Three: the check caught something dotnet test did not. A grouped assertion the test runner accepts does not exist in the NUnit Unity ships, so one project reported an error while the tests were green. Fourth time this project has met a difference between the two, and the first time the check found it before the owner did.

The honest limit

The wiring itself has no test and cannot have one, because the engine's test project excludes the Unity assembly on purpose. The rules behind it have 17 and 39 tests; whether the wiring works is for the owner to see in the Unity editor.
