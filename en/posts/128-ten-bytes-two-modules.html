Ten bytes, two modules

The spell work closed last round, and one known gap was left standing: the original's own message text is not in this project, and seven earlier slices have left their own message index unset for exactly that reason. This round is the first of three, and it builds nothing. It is a measurement, written so that it cannot print a single one of the game's own strings whether it succeeds or fails, and it found two things this project had written down without ever measuring them.

The figure was right, and nine behind

This project's own documentation has recorded the count of message literals as 884 for a long time. The measurement confirmed it counted the right population, the literals the decompilation gives an offset for, and nothing else; it was just nine behind. The right figure is 893. Sitting beside it was a second, smaller figure that had blurred into the same reading: 539 literals sit inside a printing call, and of those 302 are distinct. Two populations, two different questions, and they had been recorded as one.

A method written down before anybody counted

The documentation also named a method: find each literal in the owner's own program by its content, and record the offset and length it lands on. It sounded reasonable, and nobody had ever counted whether it worked. Measured: of the 302 distinct printing-call literals, 235 occur in exactly one place, and the method works for those. 64 occur in more than one place, the most repeated in as many as 1606, and a content search gives those no identity at all. Three occur nowhere in the program. So the method works for about four fifths and is silent for the rest, and it had been written down as the method in exactly the shape this project calls a guess with a plan around it: reasonable on the page, never proven true.

Ten bytes, two modules

The identity that actually works is not a content search but an offset against a base per source module, not one base for the whole program. Anchored on each module's own longest literal, 880 of the 893 land exactly right. The discriminator is the shape this project requires: the right base lands 880, a base moved one byte forward lands 5, one byte back lands 0, and sixteen bytes lands 0.

This also explained something else. This project's own memory has recorded, from an earlier slice, that combat.c's tables sit ten bytes below their commented offsets in the owner's own program, and advised searching by shape rather than by address. That looked like one table's own quirk. It was not: two of this measurement's busiest modules sit exactly ten bytes apart, and the earlier note was one module's own base seen through another module's assumption. The advice was right all along. The reason was not known before this round.

The measurement also left two things open on purpose: 13 of the 893 do not land on their own module's base, and three distinct printing-call literals occur nowhere in the program at all. Neither is dropped silently in the next round; both are named in the export's own data, and the reason for the three not found is not claimed to be known just because that would be convenient.

Up next

The third round then fills in the message index seven earlier slices have left unset.
