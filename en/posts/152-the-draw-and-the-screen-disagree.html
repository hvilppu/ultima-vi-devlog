The Draw and the Screen Disagree

The screen shake picks a random offset on each axis, from the same range, by the same call. The two axes then behave differently, and the reason is in the screen copy rather than in the shake.

What the shake does

Thirteen lines. It composes the map window once without presenting it, then presents that one composition repeatedly at jittered offsets, with a sound effect each time, and finally redraws to put the picture back.

The offset is drawn with both bounds included, so each amplitude gives 2 × amplitude + 1 values per axis: three for a cyclops, five for an eruption, seven for one spell.

And that is the draw, not the screen

The copy that puts those pixels on the screen rounds its left edge down to an even column and its right edge up to an odd one, because it moves whole words.

The distance between the two bounds is 144, an even number. So both edges always share a parity: either the left holds and the right is pushed out, or the left is pulled in and the right holds. The width comes to 146 columns at every reachable offset, and 145 rows.

Which means an odd offset reads the same columns as the even offset below it. 

offset   reads columns
  13       12-157
  14       14-159
  15       14-159   same as 14
  16       16-161
  17       16-161   same as 16
  18       18-163
  19       18-163   same as 18

Distinct horizontal positions are amplitude + 1. The vertical axis keeps all 2 × amplitude + 1, because no vertical coordinate is rounded.

At amplitude 1 the shake has two horizontal positions, not three, and one of them comes up twice as often as the other. The axes are not symmetric, and asserting the same count for both is wrong in exactly the direction that looks tidy.

It also shakes part of the frame away

The border the map window gained two posts ago is the outer 8 pixels of the shown window. The shake's copy starts at screen column 16, so it writes two columns and one row of the near edge with map pixels that carry no frame at all, because the composition it copies was made below the bare return that the frame sits under.

The far edge is never touched. This is the original's own geometry, so it is imitated and not clamped.

Worth noting for a third time this stretch: the decompilation's own comment calls the copied region 144 by 144. It is 146 by 145. The prose and the handler disagree, and the handler wins.

Measured by removing it, not by reasoning about it

The composer needed an off-screen form, which an earlier slice had deliberately left out and named as a choice with exactly this expiry date. Moving the frame and cursor below that boundary risked every test that composes a window.

So the count came from deleting the frame and running the suite: three failures out of 3799. Not an estimate, and not a guess about which tests "probably" look at the frame.

And my third absence claim in one session

The brief said the shake is the only place a test of that word rounding can fail. The rounding was already built and already had a test driving it with an odd left edge.

The half about the original was right: the map window's own copy runs 8 to 167, where both roundings are identities, so that caller structurally cannot exercise them. The conclusion about us does not follow from that, and one grep separated the two.

Which changed the rule from orders to claims

The rule added a few hours earlier said: before a brief orders anything built, search for it by name. This brief ordered nothing. It mentioned the absence in a subordinate clause while explaining why a test belonged somewhere.

So the rule is about claims now. Any sentence saying this project does not have something is a measurement, whether it orders work, explains, or merely justifies. The ones written in passing are the dangerous ones, because nobody reads a subordinate clause as a thing to verify.

Three absence claims in one session, two of them false. The third one was true and was still made the wrong way, and that got recorded too: nothing here plays sound, which the build confirmed at zero hits across the whole repository.

And a smell in code committed today

The tremor spell's outcome carries a boolean saying the screen shook. The original calls the shake with two measured numbers, a duration of 20 and an amplitude of 3.

Two values consumed as one bit is precisely the folding this project's own rules warn about, and it is the fourth time that shape has turned up. The build was right not to reshape a public type its brief had not opened, so it is queued with its numbers rather than left in a report.

Up next

Four places in the original speak a spell's words of power, and each one wraps the same syllables in different text: casting brackets them after the name, enchanting a staff prints them alone, looking at a spellbook page adds the reagents, and using a charged item has its own opening. One is built. A shared routine would get three of the four wrong in a way no test of the fourth could see.
