A witness shouted, now somebody comes

Theft and murder both already found a witness who shouts for help, and nothing happened after that: the shout was only ever reported. This slice built what the shout actually triggers, from two separate call sites, and found along the way that a piece that looked unbuilt had already been measured somewhere else.

Two routines, two different bounds

Raising the alarm walks every one of the 256 actor slots, starting at 0. The theft's own witness search walks from 1 up to 224 only, exclusive: it can never see slot 0, or any temporary monster slot. Two different routines in the same original source file, two different bounds, and a port that shared one bound between the two would be wrong in one direction or the other: either the alarm would never reach slot 0, or the witness search would start seeing creatures it could never see in the original.

Two distances, two karma figures, neither shared

A guard within 32 tiles of the party is arrested, and nothing else is considered for it. Every other neutral actor that is not immobile or motionless, not incapacitated and not dragged under, reacts within 6 tiles: one already on a schedule becomes a vigilante, everyone else becomes afraid. The alarm itself costs five karma, always, separate from the theft's own one: a caught theft costs six in total, as two separate subtractions, never one number tidied into another.

Busy is the band between two markers

The original tests first whether an actor is on the schedule side of the mode byte at all, and only then whether it is still looking for a path. That second test is the one that decides busy: a settled mode at the top of the schedule range, such as standing or sleeping, is not busy and gets the vigilante assignment, which is the whole point of the routine. A gate that skipped everything from the schedule marker upward would skip exactly that.

Human was measured, not guessed

The murder call site needs to know whether the victim is a human class, and this looked like an unbuilt primitive at first. It was not: an earlier slice had already found the missing human flag in the class table and measured fourteen of its sixty four classes as carrying it, the guard among them. The reader already existed before this slice; it just was not wired to anything yet.

What this does not cover

The murder path's full rule also sets the victim's alignment from the attacker's own, sets a scheduled victim to a vigilante mode instead, and additionally sets a temporary monster to an assault mode. None of that second half is built: this slice only checked the one gate that decides whether the alarm is called at all, not what happens to the victim afterward. A named gap, not a guess. This paragraph originally said the rule flips the victim's own alignment. That was wrong when it was written, and a later measurement showed it: the new value is not derived from the victim at all, but from the attacker.
