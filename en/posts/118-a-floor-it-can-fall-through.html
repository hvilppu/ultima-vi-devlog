A floor it can fall through

The equipment view has a cursor this project has never needed before: not the inventory grid, but the view's own cursor, seven columns wide, of which the pack is four and the paper doll three. It is the last piece pickpocket's second selection has been waiting on, and measuring it found two things that look like bugs and one that is.

A body that is not a slot

The paper doll's middle square, column one, row one, is not a slot at all. A move that would stop there applies the same step a second time and carries on in the direction it was already going. On a straight direction the cursor lands one square further; on a diagonal, two squares along the same diagonal. The body always sits comfortably inside the middle of the box, so this second step never itself lands outside it: nothing has to re-run the edge logic after the skip.

Two rules three lines apart, not the same shape

The pack's own edge has two ways to leave it, and they are not symmetrical. Leaving past the bottom scrolls the pack down, but only when the pressed direction is exactly down: the test compares the direction number straight against due south's own value. Leaving past the top scrolls up and does not test the direction at all, only whether anything is already scrolled past. A player holding the cursor at the pack's bottom edge and pressing a diagonal down gets nothing; a player doing exactly the same at the top edge gets the scroll. Two conditions, three lines apart in the original, and the second is simply shorter than the first rather than its mirror.

This is a different situation from this project's earlier comment-versus-code disagreements, the wizard eye's own forty against forty one and the typed line's escape value. There, the compiled game behaves exactly the same every time, and the conflict sits between the decompiler's own comment and the code, somewhere the player never looks. Here there are two real, playable paths with different outcomes from one key press, and the difference sits in the original's own code between two tests. Observable from the screen, not only from the source: this project's own bug file draws its line exactly where that observable behavior sits, so it is recorded there.

A floor it can fall through, a ceiling it cannot

The outer gate for the edge cases reads literally: the cases are tried when the new column is in the pack's own range, or the new row is past the bottom. The second half of that carries no column restriction at all. The consequence: leaving straight down from a worn side column reaches the scroll and the buttons row exactly as leaving from a pack column would, because the bottom test never asks which column the move came from. Leaving straight up from a worn side column reaches nothing, because only the pack's own columns satisfy the gate's first half. The worn side has a floor it can fall through and a ceiling it cannot, and that is the original's own test, not this project's reading of the wording: measuring it afterward confirmed the same asymmetry straight out of the code.

One flag, two gates open and two closed

For pickpocket's own flag, two of the four edge cases are gated and two are not. Walking another character's pack, the cursor cannot reach the container slot or the view's own buttons row, but can still scroll the pack in both directions. Scrolling a victim's pack is allowed; touching their container or the view's buttons is not. A rule, not a side effect of ordering: the scroll cases are tried before the container and buttons cases, and the flag is only tested in the latter two.

Still named, not yet wired

The cursor exists and is measured, but no part of the view calls it yet. Pickpocket's second selection still takes the refusal every time, because the sub-mode that shows a victim's pack, the hit test that turns a column and a row into an object, the tab glue between aiming at the map and this cursor, and where the cursor starts are all still missing. The count of spells that actually reach their handler in play does not move with this slice.
