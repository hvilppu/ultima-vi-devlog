The test that was turned inside out

The fifth and last round wired replicate, the missile family's last unbuilt target. The prediction held in full: nothing new to measure, not one line of code changed in the view. But the fifth round is not just one more after the previous four. It is the one that closes the whole set, and that shows up best in one test that no longer had anything left to claim.

Two old measurements, now through the live switch

Replicate shares its gettable gate with vanish, and slice F34 measured, long ago, that the two refuse through it with different values: replicate with one, vanish with two. Nothing in the code explains the difference, and this round repeated it without smoothing it over, but now asserted through the live missile switch rather than only inside replicate's own test suite. The same round reconfirmed that the eight-try placement window is the party's own map centre, never the copied target's own square: a test places the target nine hundred squares away from the party and still expects success, because the window never looks at the target at all.

The test that had nothing left to claim

While only one of the missile family's 23 targets was still unbuilt, the project carried a test that pinned exactly that one: an assertion that number 0x58 is the one exception with no built effect. This round wired that last one, and the test had nothing left to claim. There were two ways to handle that: delete it quietly, or turn it into its own negation. The project chose the second. The test now runs across all 23 targets and asserts the same thing every time: not one of them is still missing a built effect. Two other tests, which pinned replicate specifically as unwired, were deleted outright, because their claim had turned false and there was no sensible negation left for them to carry.

A family that started at twelve

The missile family's own switch grew across five earlier slices: the first twelve targets, four more, two more, and then five targets that were measured and built but never wired at all, because the switch never had the inputs they needed in hand. This slice, across its own five rounds, measured none of those five again. It widened the switch's signature on the first round and then wired one target at a time after that: vanish, dispel field, animate, clone, replicate. Two rounds checked, against the code rather than by assumption, that dispel field's own exemption from the target kind gate does not reach the other three targets. Neither check changed the answer, but either one could have.

Two numbers with different stories

Of the missile family's 23 targets, all 23 are now built and wired, zero left. But of the eighty spells, 78 reach their own handler in play, not 80, and this last round did not move that number at all. The first round raised it from 76 to 78 by wiring vanish and opening reappear at the same time. The other four rounds were never part of that particular count, even though they produced exactly the same outcome in play as the two spells still blocked today: dispel field, animate, clone and replicate all answered "nothing happens" for as long as they stayed unwired, the same line wizard eye still answers. Two separate counters measured partly different things the whole time, and both readings are correct on their own terms: the missile switch's own wiring counter is full now, and the whole-game counter did not move from this slice at all. What is left is the same two spells as after the first round, wizard eye and pickpocket, neither one waiting on an unmeasured spell rule, only on a view interaction mode that has not been built yet.
