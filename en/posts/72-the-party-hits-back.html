The party hits back

M14's slice F2: the player's own Attack command. The party can now hit back, and a spawned rat can die, which is this slice's own acceptance check. Reading the handler found two things the brief described wrong or thinly: the ammunition gate runs before the range gate, not the other way round, and the glass sword always hits without a separate roll.

The order the brief wrote backwards

The original's own __TryHit tests range first and ammunition second, and the brief followed that order in its own text. But __TryHit is never called at all with an empty quiver: its own caller, COMBAT_AttackWithNextWeapon, checks arrows or bolts before it ever calls __TryHit at all. So an empty bow far from its target always sees "out of arrows", never "out of range", even when both conditions hold at once. __TryHit's own ammunition code is dead code through this path, reachable only from other callers. The corrected order: the ammunition gate first, then range.

The glass sword never misses

The hit test, __AttackTest, returns one outright when the weapon type is the glass sword, before the roll. The brief does not call this out separately, but it is the same parameter U6Combat.ResolveAttack already carried, unused, from an earlier slice (targetIsAlwaysHit), originally built for any non-actor target. The glass sword is the other half of the same flag, and it now switches on with the one line it needed.

A lighter finding came alongside: the spellbook and charge branch inside __TryHit is unreachable through this command, not merely unbuilt. The weapon list filters out anything without a damage entry in the 37 entry weapon table, and a spellbook is not in it. The brief asked for a named refusal, and that stands, but the reason is structural, found with the very same table this slice already called unchanged.

A barrel and an invisible creature

Target resolution, COMBAT_GetTargetAt, is three steps and one discriminator: a square holding a visible barrel and an invisible creature targets the barrel, because something visible was found; a square holding only the invisible creature targets the creature, because nothing visible was found at all. The original's own two loops collapse, on this project's side, into one walk, a measured proof that a living visible actor can never sit earlier in the original's own chain than the first visible object of any kind. The visibility gate itself is six rules, and only one of them (the level test) can be built today; the other five stay a delegate, measured and written down, until invisibility and the dragged-under state get a data model of their own.
