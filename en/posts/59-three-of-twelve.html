Three of Twelve

M14's third slice continued with the four positioning modes that read slice C1's own geometry: AI_FRONT, AI_REAR, AI_FLANK and AI_BERSERK. Three of them pick a target by their own rule, and they agree in exactly three of twelve cells.

Three rules, twelve cells, three in common

Assault, flank and berserk each choose a target by a different rule, and over the owner's own 64 creature classes they disagree more often than they agree: 

actor         assault     flank   berserk
NEUTRAL             0        52        64
EVIL               35        35         2
GOOD               50        50        17
CHAOTIC            19        31        19

Each of the three differences carries its own story. A good berserker ignores all 33 chaotic classes, the game's commonest alignment, because the clause is an equality against EVIL rather than the attack-player bit: a berserk party member attacks almost nothing a front one would charge. Nobody has seen this in play, so it waits in originalBugs.md with a mechanism, not a fix. A chaotic flanker attacks neutrals, because the flank rule has no equivalent of the third filter assault needs. A neutral berserker attacks anybody, its own side included, because all three of the berserk rule's clauses test for an alignment it does not have. A port sharing one targeting function between the three modes would get at least two of them wrong, and no test would fail unless the table above is in the suite itself.

Berserk pays twice

Every other mode pays for what it does: an attack costs 10, a wait costs 5, a failed move costs nothing. Berserk additionally spends 5 unconditionally at the end of every path, whether it fired, sidestepped, or assaulted instead for lack of a target. A shot that lands costs 15 where the same shot costs 10 everywhere else, and a berserk step costs the terrain plus 5.

A flank mapping that is crossed on purpose

AI_REAR stays between its own flanks, but the party's and the enemies' flank maps are crossed: positive is left for the party, negative is left for the enemies. The test built a scenario on purpose where the same actor's own side value is stuck under the correct reading and would have moved under the wrong one: the actor stays put with the real mapping, and the wrong mapping would have fired its very first comparison instead. One mapping for both sides would mirror the enemies' own back line, which looks like a sensible formation on the field and is the wrong one.

A walk whose price does not depend on the terrain

AI_FLANK's early exit walks toward the Avatar when the actor is far away, but the walk's own points are restored right afterward and a flat 5 is charged instead. That is the opposite of every other walk in the game, where the terrain decides the price.

The sentinel again

AI_FLANK's own target scan picks whichever candidate sits furthest outward, starting from int16's 0x8000, the same trap slice C1 measured in PartyFrontMember. Widening that start to a positive value would not crash anything; it would just quietly stop taking the first candidate, on any map at all.
