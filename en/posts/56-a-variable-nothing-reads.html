A Variable Nothing Reads

The next piece of M14's third slice is now measured and built: ComputeCombatGeometry, which runs at the top of every combat turn to work out the party's and the enemies' own gravity centres, plus a front member and four flank slots. One of the five selected actors turned out to be something nothing in the whole decompilation ever reads, and it still gets recomputed every single turn.

The rounding term is the party size, not the number counted

The party's own gravity centre skips anyone flanking or berserk when summing positions, but the rounding still adds the whole party's size rather than however many actually got summed. On the shipped party, with one of four excluded, the original gives (307, 354) and the tidy num >> 1 reading would give (307, 353): one square off, and the whole front line shifts with it. Exhaustively over every party size from one to eight and every count, the two readings differ on at least one coordinate sum whenever anybody is excluded, and the fault is reachable with one keystroke: the player sets a member to flank from the status line mid fight.

-32768, not +32768

The front member search compares every participant against a running maximum that starts at 0x8000 read as a signed 16 bit integer, -32768. A port that widens the variable to 32 bits would give it +32768 instead, and the result is not a smaller error but no answer at all: on the shipped party, in all three measured axis directions, the front member is never assigned even once, so the variable would be stuck forever at whatever the last turn that still beat it left there. A wrong width looks harmless until it turns out it does not give a wrong answer, it stops giving any answer.

A variable nothing reads

Of the six selected actors, five travel onward: the consumers read the enemies' own front member and flanks, and the party's own flanks. The sixth, PartyFrontMember, is computed with exactly the same dot product as the rest of the party's own results, but across the whole decompilation it has one single write and zero reads. No combat state handler, not COMBAT_MoveToFrontLine, nothing else ever asks for its value. It is implemented here exactly as the original computes it, not as nought and not skipped, but nothing in this project may consume it: the wrong move here would not be computing it, it would be using it, which would be inventing behaviour the original does not have.

Participation is a strict filter, not the whole party

Only AI_FRONT and AI_COMMAND take part in the search, and on the shipped party the third member (AI_REAR) never does, even though his own coordinates would win the search if he did. The flank threshold starts at nought and the comparison is strict, so a participant sitting exactly on the axis is never a flank: on the shipped party the Avatar's own side is exactly nought, and both flank slots fill with the two other members instead. The enemies' own front slot, despite its name, is not the most forward enemy at all, it is the lowest slot number among those taking part.
