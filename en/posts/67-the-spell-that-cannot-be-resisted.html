The spell that cannot be resisted

The winds, the party-ring spells and the summons, M14's slice D7, the last three of the spell dispatcher map's eight shared handlers. One of them turned up a mistake in the previous slice's own claim along the way: energy wind, which was supposed to behave like the other two direct damage spells, runs through no resistance check of any kind.

The dispatcher was read while measuring something else

The previous slice, D4, claimed direct damage serves three spells, harm, disable and energy wind, all three running through the same combat contest. That is wrong for one of the three. SPELL_DirectDamage has exactly two call sites in the whole dispatcher, the missile cast's switch for harm and the object cast's switch for disable, and none at all for energy wind. The dispatcher sends energy wind to its own wind handler instead, which rolls its own 1d30 directly, with no resistance test. The two routines happen to roll the same 1d30, which is exactly why the difference never showed: from the outside, both look the same.

Energy wind is therefore the one damaging spell in this group that cannot be resisted at all. The correction turned up as a side effect of measuring the wind handler, not as a measurement of its own, and it was proved by seed identity: a contest roll is one draw from the shared generator, and one draw shifts every later roll by one position. The same seed fed to two parallel generators, one given the extra contest draw and one not, shows the difference directly: they land on different points of the same sequence.

Three handlers, three different answers to success

The missile cast prints a success message and plays a sound. The mass and field handlers print the message without playing anything. The wind handler does neither: no message, no sound, the loop ends and the handler returns. The party-ring spells do a fourth thing, printing and playing a sound always, unconditionally, regardless of what the per-victim loop did to anyone. Four handlers built so far, four different answers to the same question, and none of them have been unified at any point.

Confuse takes away an opponent, not gives one

Among the party-ring spells, confuse does not turn enemies against each other, despite what the name suggests. It sets the target's mode to assault but also its alignment to neutral, and slice C2 had already measured that a neutral actor finds zero valid targets in the opponent search's own logic, unless it is in the special vigilante mode. A confused creature therefore attacks nobody. This was tested here directly through the real opponent search, run side by side against the same situation with the alignment left untouched, where the latter does find a target: the only difference is that one write.

Two rolls, one unused

Among the summoning spells, conjure picks one of five creatures with a single roll of 0 to 15, weighted 4, 3, 3, 3, 3 of 16, not a uniform fifth. Summon takes the same roll but never uses it: it always calls a daemon. The roll is taken before the spell is even examined, so casting summon consumes a creature type it never uses. Invisible in play, visible from a seed: the same seed fed to both spells produces exactly the same placement attempts, which would differ if summon skipped the draw. If all eight placement attempts in an 11 by 11 box fail, the creature is deleted silently: only the failure sound plays, no message, where every other failure in this and the earlier slices says something.
