Ten bytes past the comments

M16's last open item: the red gates' destination tables. They are three tables in the owner's own program, and the decompilation records their offsets. The offsets are ten bytes off, and proving that was the whole job.

Where the position comes from

The module base comes from npctrack.c's own string literals, exactly as the mantras take objcmd.c's: the literals' offsets are recorded and their landing proves the base. Read against that base, though, the data tables are not where their offsets point. They are ten bytes further on.

Ten is the kind of number that has to be proved rather than assumed, because a wrong reading produces numbers that look like data.

A scored discriminator, and why coordinates alone will not do

Three structural claims: a map coordinate is ten bits (below 1024), a level is 0 to 5, and rows 11 to 13 are nought in both coordinate tables. The score walks every shift from -12 to +20: 

shift    score (max 78)
   ...
    +6      73
    +8      76   <- 50 coordinates, 24/25 levels, 2/3 nought rows
   +10      78   <- all of it
   +12      76
   +14      74
   ...

Only +10 scores full marks. And here is the part that makes the score a measurement rather than decoration: every shift from +8 to +20 gets all 50 coordinates into a plausible range. A discriminator built from coordinates alone would not separate the right reading from its neighbours at all, and coordinates plus levels would separate it by a single point. The three nought rows are what make the gap two.

The quality is one past its row, and nought is not a refusal

A gate's own quality names the destination: gateIdx = GetQual(obj); gateIdx--;, so quality 1 is the first row. And quality nought is not "no destination" but a real teleport to the square already stood on, which in the original means running the same routine with its own regrouping. A reading that made nought a refusal would leave the party unformed.

Three rows lead to the map's own corner

Rows 11, 12 and 13 are nought in all three tables. They are most plausibly unused slots in a table of 25 of which the plot uses 22. But the original does not test them at all: a gate whose quality was 12 would teleport the party to (0, 0).

This implementation does not test them either. Adding a refusal would be inventing a rule, and those three rows are themselves part of the discriminator that found the tables. Recorded as an anomaly.

And what this is not: playable

Nought of the 16838 objects in the owner's save carries the red gate's type. The gates arrive through the plot, so nobody can stand on one until the plot makes one. This slice is therefore not visible in play, and that is said out loud in the code, in the plan and here: the rule is in place for M17.

The same was true of the amulet of submission two slices ago: the rule exists, the object does not yet.
