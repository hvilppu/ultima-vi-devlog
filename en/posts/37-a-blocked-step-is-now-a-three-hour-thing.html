A Blocked Step Is Now a Three Hour Thing

Movement points are in the engine now: how many steps a character gets, depending on its own speed and the terrain. The original ticks by the minute, this project by the hour, and that difference just showed up in one place no amount of measuring could have predicted ahead of time.

The formula is not the resistance map's formula

SubTerrainMov computes what leaving one square costs: 5 plus the terrain's own nibble plus every object standing on the square's own nibble, impassable tiles skipped rather than blocking anything. The pathfinding resistance map reads a different number from the same data, 1 + nibble, with its own special cases for doors and bridges. They are not the same number, and reusing one for the other would have been an easy shortcut to take.

Measured in the Avatar's own window: of 1122 walkable squares, 1107 match resistance + 4, and 15 do not: 13 doors and 2 wall objects. A door costs 15 where resistance + 4 would say 6, because the resistance map gives every door a flat +1 and SubTerrainMov knows nothing about doors at all, it just sums the door tile's own nibble.

A test found a phantom square

The first reading claimed 16 exceptions, 13 doors and three wall objects in three neighbouring squares. A test that walks the whole window one square at a time instead of reading a named list of three found that the middle square has no object on it at all. The two neighbours invited assuming a third between them, and that assumption was wrong: 15 exceptions, two wall objects. The same mistake this project has made before in other shapes: a named example is easier to believe than measuring it again.

Blocking moved from the minute to the hour, and that changed what three tries means

In the original a blocked step zeroes the whole point balance, not merely subtracts from it. The three-try blocking escalation (wait, wait again, give up) plays out over three minutes in the original, because the grant happens every minute whenever nobody has points left. This project keeps the hourly round instead: the grant happens once an hour, not once a minute.

Consequence: once the zeroing rule was implemented exactly as measured, three existing tests failed. Their expectation was that no points were needed for a second or third consecutive try, which held only because movement points did not exist yet. The first instinct was to ask whether the rule should be softened for the tests' sake. The answer was no: a measured rule is implemented exactly, and the tests get fixed to match it. Three tests now run the hourly round between two tries, the same way a minute would pass in the original. The escalation now plays out hour by hour rather than player step by player step, and that is the correct consequence of this architectural choice, not a fault to paper over.

What stayed unbuilt on purpose

Characters standing on a square do not add to its cost, even though the original would count them too: the measured effect is nought across all 200 placed characters in the owner's own save, because every one of them stands on either an impassable tile or one with nibble nought. A square-indexed character lookup for an effect proven to be zero was left out as a decision. The party leader's and followers' own movement (PartyAdvance, MoveFollowers) and vehicles are still waiting their turn.
