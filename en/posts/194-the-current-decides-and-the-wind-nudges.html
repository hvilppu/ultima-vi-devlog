The current decides and the wind nudges

M16 had two items left open: the raft's drift table and the wind's random change. Both come out of one function and both sit behind keys the player already presses, so for once the slice was chosen by what is wrong in play rather than by what comes next in the plan's numbering.

The raft sailed straight downwind, which is up to four steps wrong

What was built was the wind half: a raft and a balloon do not steer, they go where the wind blows. But the original has a table, RaftDriftDir[current][wind], and counting all 64 of its entries gives the rule in one sentence: 

the current's own direction          32 entries
one eighth turn from it              32 entries
further away than one                 0 entries

The current decides and the wind nudges. A wind blowing straight against the current leaves the current's direction in all eight cases, and the table's diagonal is the identity. So a raft sent straight downwind can be four steps wrong.

And the half that was not there at all

What "the current" means depends on the square. It is read off the tile when that tile is one of the eight current tiles. On any other water the original puts the direction the player asked for in the current's place. A raft on open water is therefore steerable to within one eighth turn, and the same key gives a different step on a current than off it.

This is the "four states read as two" smell this project's own rules warn about: one generalisation had been read out of a 64 entry table.

A becalmed balloon could fly

The original computes dir = WindDir ^ 4 on a signed value. Calm is -1, and -1 ^ 4 is -5, so it stays negative and reaches the refusal: the line prints, the movement points are zeroed, and the turn is gone. Our view read "no wind" as "keep the key's direction", which is the raft's rule and not the balloon's.

Reaching for (WindDir + 4) & 7 instead would have turned calm into direction 3 and flown a becalmed balloon north east. That is now a test's discriminator.

And the wind turns now, which is draw accounting

UpdateWind runs once per call and not once per minute: PassTime loops over minutes for the status effects and calls this outside that loop, so a ten minute rest takes one wind draw rather than ten. The random stream is shared, so how many draws a pass takes is part of the rule: 

underground, wind blowing    forced calm, 0 draws
underground, already calm    1 draw, and nothing happens
on the surface, no bite      1 draw
on the surface, a bite       2 draws
noChange (the sky bar)       0 draws

The second line is the interesting one. The forcing's condition requires a blowing wind, so an already calm one falls through it, takes its one-in-64 draw and is then refused by the surface test. A draw taken for an outcome that cannot happen, reproduced rather than tidied, because tidying it would move every later draw in the game.

A new wind is OSI_rand(0, 8) - 1, nine outcomes, so the wind can also drop and calm is no rarer than any single direction. Level 5, incidentally, is not underground to this rule: the forcing is z < 5 and the draw is z == 0 || z == 5.

A comment that was wrong in every clause

U6Wind's own documentation said the ambient change fires at a finer grain than anything this project ties its clock to, so it is not modelled, and that the Wind Change spell does not exist either "since no spell system has been built".

The grain was one line of gameloop.c away. The spell had been built. And nine spell families have landed since that sentence was written. An absence claim in a comment is the sentence nobody updates, and this is the second slice in a row where it cost something: the previous one was five conversation members. The correction is written in place, with what the comment used to say kept beside it.
