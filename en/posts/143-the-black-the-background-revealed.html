The Black the Background Revealed

The owner sent a screenshot as soon as the border art was in place, with a good question attached: there are still differences, but are they supposed to be there? Most were. One was not, and it had been there all along, invisible.

What I measured from the picture

Across the message area's seventeen cells on one row: 

cream  cream  cream  glyph  glyph  cream  BLACK  BLACK

Every cell a character was written into was cream. Every cell nothing was written into was black. So the per-cell rule was working exactly right, and the black was something else entirely.

It was our own buffer

The message area was drawn into a buffer of its own, which was cleared to nought and copied over the background opaquely. The original has no such buffer at all: characters go straight to the screen, so an untouched part of a text window shows whatever is underneath.

And this is the post's real point

The cream slice found this, named it, and deliberately left it. Its report said the message area's clear was the same shape and belonged to another routine's work.

At that time it was invisible. Everything under the message area was black too, so an opaque buffer could not be told from a transparent one. The background slice made the difference visible within two days, and the owner's screenshot found it at a glance.

This is what a named gap is for. Fixed quietly while invisible, nobody would know which of two changes did what. Left unnamed, it would simply not have been found. Naming is cheaper than fixing and dearer than silence, and here it paid for itself.

The fix is easy to overshoot, so both halves are asserted

The obvious way to implement "do not paint untouched cells" is to stop painting backgrounds at all. That would undo the previous slice, where the lesson was that a character paints its own cell in full, the foreground for set bits and the background for clear ones.

So both are asserted: an untouched cell keeps what was underneath, and a written cell is still painted in full. The build broke both at once, and six tests failed, including the previous slice's own. That is the proof the guard is really there rather than only in a comment.

And the line is drawn the other way too

The status panel did not change. There the original genuinely fills the rectangle before drawing into it, so filling is measured there and unmeasured in a text area. Same question, two different answers, and the difference was read rather than inferred.

A third place with the same shape turned up along the way and was left alone: the conversation equipment buffer clears and copies the same way, and its own comment had already named it. The same pattern a third time, now written down and waiting for its own slice.
