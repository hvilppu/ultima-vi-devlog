Notes on a 1:1 reimplementation in C# and Unity.

The night was too bright

The visibility fill is wired into the map window now, so light stops at a wall, a cave no longer shows through its own walls, and a wall seen from one side gets its own face. Wiring it also showed that the shape of the lit area has been wrong from the start, and that one was mine rather than the original's.

A square where the original has a circle

A square's light is 4 - distance + daylight. That formula was measured correctly and has been right for a long time. But distance in this project was the greater of the two offsets, so a diagonal step cost nothing and the lit area was a square. The original's light distance is an eight by eight table, and it is round. 

of the window's 121 squares, how many differ            64

lit squares in the window         square        table
  daylight 0                        49           37
  daylight 1                        81           61
  daylight 2                       121           89
  daylight 3                       121          109
  daylight 4 and above             121          121

So the night was too bright at every hour below four, and the wrong shape at all of them. In daylight the two agree, which is why nobody caught it: a torch's own floor is four, so the picture was right with a torch too.

The formula stayed, the distance went, and the class that carried it now keeps the sky and the shading and does not own a square's light at all.

Three passes, and a wall is visible because the marking comes before the test

The wiring runs three passes per composition: the objects' flags, the party's own fill with the ambient light, and the eleven by eleven view build. That order is the original's, and so is the detail that a square is marked and lit before the see-through test. That is the whole reason a wall is visible at all: the fill reaches the wall, lights it, marks it, and only then declines to spread through it. The other order would give a cave of corridors with no walls.

Counting the x-ray flag's setters found a consumer nobody had opened

The original has a flag meaning "see through walls": the telescope's scroll sets it, the x-ray spell sets it, and the wizard eye sets it, three places counted. The branch skips the whole fill. I wired the flag from the eye, since that is the one of the three this project has.

And counting those setters turned up a second reader, in another file, that had never been opened. It does two things this build does not:

  • it draws the shade after the objects, not before, so a chest in a dim square is shaded in the original and is not here
  • it hides whatever stands on a wall the party cannot see from its own side

The first is a correction of its own, and the awkward part is this: the shading class's remarks have cited that very routine since they were written. What was measured there was the light value. The order was five lines further down the same loop, and nobody read those five lines.

The test found its own spot, and its first claim was refuted

The wiring's test compares two compositions, one with the fill and one with the x-ray branch. The first position I picked hid nothing inside the window, because the party sees its own room, so the test now sweeps 400 dungeon positions and measures where the difference is largest.

Its first claim was that every difference is a hidden square. It met 0x10B, a wall corner: the third of the three faults turning up in the measurement of the other two. The classification stayed in the test. 

dungeon positions swept                                   400
positions where the fill hides something                  297
squares hidden in all                                   14460
wall faces swapped                                          1

Exactly one wall face across four hundred positions. The replacement is that rare in a dungeon, because it needs a wall whose neighbours keep both connection gates shut and whose four sides are not all visible. So the replacement is driven by its own test file rather than by this sweep, and that single one is pinned only so that losing it entirely would still break something.

What is still owed

The light source pass. A lamp or a brazier standing in the world lights nothing; a torch the party carries does, because it raises the ambient. It is measured and written down, not built. The same goes for the shade's drawing order and for hiding what stands on an unseen wall.
