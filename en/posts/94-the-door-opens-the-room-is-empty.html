The door opens, the room is empty

The previous slice opened seven doors: a spell number now finds its own handler family. This slice's job was to call that handler and make a spell actually happen in the world. It calls it now, from the view itself. But the door opens onto an empty room: not one of the seven families knows which of its own spells was just cast.

One number, many rooms in the same house

Each of the seven handlers covers more than one spell: eight duration spells in one shape, nine area effects in another, four winds, four fields, four party-ring effects, two summons. The dispatcher answers "which family does this number belong to", but never "which of the family's own spells is this number". That second question would need reading every handler by name, one spell at a time, and this project carries no spell names anywhere. Seven earlier measuring slices measured what each case *does*, never which raw number it answers to. That connection has never existed.

What opened anyway

Two families, the area effects and the fields, share one opening shape that asks for no per-spell case at all: only coordinates and the three geometry checks the view already owns for the Attack command. That shape now runs for real, with real geometry, not a stand-in: a spell aimed at an empty square and a shot that misses both refuse exactly as the original refuses them. Only once the shot actually lands does the measuring run out. That is a smaller win than "the spell works", but it is a real one: for the first time, a player can see the difference between an empty square and a hit square, even though the hit does nothing yet.

Three stand-ins were enough that a fourth wasn't needed

For each of the five remaining families, it would have been tempting to pick one of its own cases as a default and run that until the real map turns up. This project has made that exact mistake three times before this slice, and three stand-ins were enough: each looked harmless at the moment it was written and turned into a real fault the moment something actually called it. A fourth was not needed. Each of the five is named whole and unwired, with its own precise reason: two would also need a routine that is not built, one would need data that was never exported, one would need a path that has never been measured at all.
