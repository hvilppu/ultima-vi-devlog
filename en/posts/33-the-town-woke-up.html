The town woke up

At twelve o'clock 36 characters set off somewhere. The schedules are in the game now, so people no longer stand where the save file left them.

The rule was already read. It was not enough

This slice was unusual in that the format and the hour rule had been written out months ago: an entry is five bytes, the hour is in the low five bits, and the match is searched for backwards so that the last one wins. I expected to be doing implementation.

I read the handler anyway before writing a line, and it corrected my first implementation in four places: 

the range is 2 to 223, not 0 to 255
a vigilante calms down BEFORE anything else
a slot with no shape is skipped
only somebody in a schedule state or retreating is governed

Not one of the four would have failed anything. Three of them would have looked like a character walking.

The last was the worst. Without it a character in the middle of a fight goes off to work when the clock strikes. Picture it in the game: the guard you are fighting turns round and walks away. Odd, but not broken, and above all not something a test would notice.

The action does not start on the hour

An entry names both a place and an action: sleep, sit, eat, play. The natural mistake is to apply the action the moment the hour turns.

The original does not. It sets the character to find a path and nothing else. The action waits for the arrival.

Had I applied it at once, at ten at night the whole town would have fallen asleep wherever it happened to be standing. In the street, in a doorway, in the middle of the market.

A number I had been repeating wrongly

I have said in this devlog and in the plan several times that 61 characters keep a daily routine. I measured it properly, and the number is 178.

Both are real numbers about different things. 61 is how many characters have a non-zero ScheduleIndex byte in the character table. 178 is how many have entries in the SCHEDULE file. The pointer table is indexed by character number and not by that byte, so 178 is the one that answers "who moves".

The number was wrong in a place where nobody questioned it, because it was right somewhere else.

What the measurement said

entries                                 563
characters with a routine               178   (numbered 5 to 190)
applies every day                       540
names one day                            23
hours in use                             23 / 24
busiest hour                             12, with 54 entries

slots in the range 2 to 223             222
of those, with no shape                  36
through the gate                        176
and with an entry at noon                36

23 hours of 24, and I asserted all of them first. The test said otherwise: nobody has anything to do at four in the morning. That was another guess written as an assertion without being measured, the third this week.

The ratio between 540 and 23 is why searching backwards is a rule rather than a detail: those 23 sit in the file after the entry they are meant to override.

The route is a stand-in, and it says so

A character is now in the right place at the right time. It gets there by a route the original would not have taken.

The original's pathfinder is PTH_BuildPath, PTH_Visit, PTH_FindPath, PTH_AddObjResist and PTH_SearchPaths, about a thousand lines of resistance map and stored paths. It has not been read. What stands in its place walks greedily one square at a time and gives up when it is boxed in.

That is a parity gap rather than a bug, and the difference is that the gap is written down: in the code, in the format document and in the plan. The real pathfinder is a known piece of work and not a surprise.

One small thing about the step is still a rule: a diagonal is one step, because the original's reach test treats a diagonal neighbour as adjacent. The distance is therefore the larger difference and not the sum of the two.

And one is a choice, which I say is a choice: among equally close steps the straighter one wins. Without it the order of the direction table decided, and a character drifted sideways down a straight corridor. There is nothing to compare it against, so it is not a measurement.

Three checks, broken on purpose

the action applied at once       2 tests failed
the search run forwards          1 test failed
