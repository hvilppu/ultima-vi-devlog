A lamp and what goes on top

The last two pieces of the visibility module: a lamp standing in the world now lights the room around it, and the shade overlay goes over the objects instead of under them. The second was a one-pass move, and it would have gone in untested, because nothing in the whole suite covered the order.

A lamp's three gates

The light source pass gives every visible square carrying a light of its own a fill of its own. Three conditions, all measured:

  • The whole pass is skipped at full daylight. A lamp adds nothing at noon.
  • A lamp must itself be visible. One behind a wall lights nothing, not even on its own side of the wall.
  • A lamp on a wall counts only when the square south of it is visible. The original writes that test twice with an && between the copies, which changes nothing, and the decompilation marks it with a question of its own.

And a fourth thing that is not a gate but a necessity: each lamp forgets the queue's marks in a seven by seven box around itself before its own fill. Without that the party's own fill, which has already marked everything it reached, would stop every lamp at its first step. A test runs the fill with the box and without it.

The gate's test was wrong twice before it was right

First my helper, which puts a lamp on a square, put the lamp's own tile on the ground. That stopped the square being a wall, and the whole wall gate became unreachable. A lamp is an object: the flags carry its light and the ground stays what it is.

And an earlier version of the same test could only assert its own setup, which is exactly the test that cannot fail. It is replaced by one that puts the party north of a lit wall: the wall is seen, the square south of it is not, and the gate closes.

The shade belongs on top, and proving it meant reading the stack

The previous post said I had found a consumer nobody had opened, and that it draws the shade after the objects. I left the move undone then, because I did not know what ShowObject's fourth argument means. It is read now, and the answer has two halves.

The routine draws nothing. It adds a tile to the cell's stack, and the stack is drawn later from the bottom up. Mode 3 walks to the end of the stack, meaning the top, but only if the tile it carries is a foreground one. Otherwise it goes to the bottom.

So the question is settled by the owner's own tile table, not by the code: 

0x1BC  the replacement   NOT foreground
0x1BD  shade 1           foreground
0x1BE  shade 2           foreground
0x1BF  shade 3           foreground

The three shades are foreground tiles, so they go on top. A chest in a dim square is shaded in the original along with the ground it stands on, and it was not here.

The test twice and the verification once wrong

The move broke no test, which is a result in itself: nothing covered the order. I wrote a test that works at the level of one pixel, and it took three attempts.

The first version passed either way. It looked for a square where every opaque overlay pixel showed the overlay, which selected precisely those squares where the object does not cover the overlay at all. The decisive pixel has to be one where both the object and the overlay are opaque.

Then the verification was wrong. I restored the old order by adding a call before the objects without removing the new one after them. The overlay was drawn twice and the later one won, so the test passed and looked as though it did not discriminate. With the move done properly it fails at pixel (9, 0): it wants the overlay's 0 and gets the object's 45.

That is three mistakes in a row on one small thing, and all three were on the test's side rather than the code's. It is why the rule here is that a new check is proved by breaking it.
