A log instead of one line

The view had one status line, and sixty two call sites that each overwrote it. One player step can give several actors a turn, and every blow wrote the same line: the player only ever saw whichever one wrote last, by accident, and three measured fields never showed up at all. This slice replaced the line with a bounded history, through one property setter rather than sixty two separate text edits, and found along the way that one of the three supposed gaps was not actually a gap.

One setter, not sixty two fixes

The brief warned directly: sixty two is a grep's count of one assignment pattern, not a census, and a writer that built its line differently would fall outside it. The answer is not sixty two separate text edits but one: the Status property's own setter now appends the line to a bounded log instead of replacing it. Nothing can write past an auto-property's own field, so every call site, old or new, goes through the same path structurally rather than by luck of a text pattern.

Two of the three missing fields really were missing

The provoke check and its own amount had been measured and tested across three earlier slices, and none of the view's three hit branches (graze, a surviving hit, a kill) ever reported it. An egg hatching's own warning direction had likewise been measured months ago, but the view never read the hatch call's own return value at all. Both now report as plain, mechanical numbers, not invented prose.

The third one was not missing after all

The brief also named a wound's own severity as one of the three invisible fields. The old code said otherwise: the default branch for an ordinary, surviving hit already wrote the severity before this slice ever touched it. "Verified earlier is a claim like any other" held here against the brief, not the code, and the finding is recorded in the plan as it stands rather than quietly cleaned away.

What this does not carry

The lines are numbers and identifiers, not the original's own wording and nothing written to sound like it: the original's own 884 string literals wait for their own, later slice and their own measurement method, the exact one this project already uses for every other piece of published text.
