A Coin That Favours the Last One

M14's third slice continues: who attacks whom (COMBAT_pickOpponent), with what (__PickWeapon), and COMBAT_AI_Assault, the mode every other attack mode falls into when it has nothing better to do. All three were already measured, with slice C0's line of fire and slice C1's geometry sitting ready, so this piece only had to call them.

The third filter is not redundant

Target choice runs five filters in order, and the third one, which rejects every neutral target, looks at first glance like it repeats the two that follow it: a GOOD actor rejects anything without the attack-player bit anyway, and EVIL rejects the other way round, and neutral carries neither bit. It earns its keep for exactly one actor: CHAOTIC. With it, a chaotic actor has 19 valid targets out of 64. Without it, 31. And the 12 extra are exactly the 12 monster classes that are themselves neutral. Dropping the filter would have looked like tidier code and made chaotic monsters start killing townspeople.

Three distances, not one

The same pair of functions uses three different ways to measure "how far": the eight tile gate is Chebyshev, max(|dx|, |dy|); the nearest target is chosen by squared Euclidean distance; and a weapon's reach is tested against the combat range table, which is neither of the above. Measuring every offset that lands inside the gate, 288 of them, found 1680 pairs that Chebyshev and squared Euclidean rank in opposite orders: one can be nearer by one measure and farther by the other at the same time. The gate itself is not a circle either: 92 offsets pass the eight tile Chebyshev gate that an equivalent Euclidean gate would reject, all of them diagonal, and none the other way round. Three measures, three different jobs, and none of them stands in for another.

A coin that favours the last one

When two targets are exactly tied for distance, the original does not pick between them evenly: it tosses a coin for every later tied candidate, one at a time, rather than once over the whole group. The result is biased in a very particular way: the last candidate scanned wins half the time no matter how many are tied. With five tied candidates the chances from first to last are 0.062, 0.062, 0.125, 0.250 and 0.500, not 0.200 each, which is what an even pick would give. A uniform reservoir pick is the implementation anyone would write first, and it is wrong.

An NPC must not cast

Weapon choice walks the actor's inventory and stops if a spellbook or a charge passes three conditions: a one in four roll, the SpellFx[13] flag clear, and a second roll that strongly favours low circles. In the original this means casting the spell. In this project an NPC cannot cast yet, so the gate is built and consumes exactly the same random draws in the same order, but refuses to pick the spellbook as a weapon instead, the same shape of refusal the player's own Attack command already uses for anything that is not a weapon. One side note from measuring it: SpellFx[13] is not only the Negate Magic spell, it stays set the entire time anyone in the party wears a storm cloak, which switches off every NPC's spellcasting for as long as it is worn.
