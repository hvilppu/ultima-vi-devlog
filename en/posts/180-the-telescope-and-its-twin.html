The telescope and its twin

The telescope was one of the objects a player still got "Not built yet." from, and on the list it was named but never opened. Opened, it turned out to be the wizard eye's twin: the same loop, the same stopping tile, the same counter, the same two screen fades and the same closing message. What is interesting is what differs, because those four differences are the discriminators.

A twin, and why the numbers were not written twice

The wizard eye is already built here. The telescope's routine is the same tail line for line: the counter starts at forty, it stops at tile 0x0FC, a see-through flag and two screen fades bracket the loop, and the same message row prints at the end. Those are read from the eye's own class rather than written again, because two copies of a measured number are two things that can drift. This project has paid for that once already in a different pair of helpers.

Four differences, and the third is the sharpest

  • The direction is asked once. The eye reads a key on every step; the telescope reads one, echoes its name, and from there the scroll runs by itself.
  • The move is spent unconditionally. The eye's own decrement sits inside its direction branch, so a player pressing other keys can scroll for ever; the telescope's is in the body.
  • The telescope always takes at least one step. Its loop variable starts at nought and the stopping tile is read at the end of the body, where the eye reads the tile under itself before the loop and can end having moved nowhere. The same loop, opposite answers from the same square, and it is tested by running both from that square with the same tile reader.
  • The scroll starts on the telescope's own square and not on the party's.

And the journey is 41 squares, not 40, because the counter is tested after it is spent. The same number the eye travels, read from its own constant rather than derived again.

The refusal leaves the map in the wrong place

Answer the direction prompt with anything else and the original prints "nowhere." and returns immediately, which is before it restores the view's own centre. So the map stays parked on the telescope.

Why that is harmless in the original: the step routine begins by setting the centre to the active party member before it computes anything, so the next step snaps it back. Checking that takes more than the line the routine shows, so I listed all 22 writers of MapX, unfiltered and untruncated. Six of them set it from the party, and none of them runs on every frame.

This is not imitated, and the reason is architectural. In this project the view's centre is the player's position and a character follows it, which was recorded back in the number-key slice. Copying the line would leave the Avatar parked on the telescope for good: neither the original's behaviour nor its outcome. Recorded as entry 3.40, together with what the refusal does not turn on, since the see-through flag and the fade both sit below that return.

What was not followed, and why half would be worse

The original raises a flag for the scroll that makes walls transparent and lights everything. It is not followed here, exactly the thinner treatment the wizard eye already gets. Half of it, lighting the window alone, would be consuming a multi-state rule as fewer states than it has, which is the class of fault this project's own rules carry four scars from. A named gap beats half a rule.
