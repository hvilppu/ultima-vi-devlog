The table whose stride was not a length

M20's fifth piece, small but complete: page 15's own played-time sentence, left open last round. Reading the decompilation to its end showed it never waited on anything from GAME.EXE at all, except the three numbers themselves.

The page's own sentence logic was already in this file

Years, months and days get worded out, and the sentence is assembled by conditions that all live in END.EXE's own main.c: a comma between the years and whatever follows, a comma and newline between months and days, "and" before the last piece when days are included. One genuine original quirk survived: when months are zero but years and days are not, both the comma and the "and" still appear, even though months are missing entirely.

A table whose stride turned up in the measuring

numberWords looked at first like the other 45 text fragments: search for the raw bytes, confirm uniqueness, record the offset. But the gaps between the thirty words were not "length plus one" apart, they were exactly 13 bytes each, matching the decompilation's own declaration char numberWords[30][13] precisely. The discriminator had to be rewritten: a structural check, a letter or a hyphen followed by zero padding, never compared against the real words themselves. The measured offset is the only one in the whole file that reaches all 30 rows; the closest wrong ones, a whole row-width either side, reach at most 29.
