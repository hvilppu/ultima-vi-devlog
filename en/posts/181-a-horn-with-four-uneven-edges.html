A horn with four uneven edges

The silver horn was marked in the plan's table as waiting for combat. It was not waiting for combat but for egg hatching and the temporary actor slots, and both have existed here for a long time. The row itself was the reason nobody opened the routine, and opened, it was cheaper than its own entry claimed. 80 of the 85 usable types now reach their own handler.

Four edges, four different distances

The horn summons up to three groups of silver serpents just outside the map window. Each try rolls an edge and an offset along it, and the edges are not symmetric: west is MapX - 6, east MapX + 7, north MapY - 7 and south MapY + 6. A port using one radius gets three of the four wrong, and no other test would say a word about it.

The asymmetry belongs with what is tested: six squares, the four the serpent covers plus one east and one north. And the test short circuits, so a try that fails on its first square costs one call rather than six, which is countable and therefore pinned.

The dice, both ends exact

draws per try                                    2

all three groups landing at once                 9 draws
all three groups failing                        48 draws

The order inside a group is edge, offset, quantity, checked against a parallel generator: rolling the quantity first would show up in no other test. And the refusal stands above every roll: if a silver serpent is already alive in any of the 32 temporary slots, a second blow costs nothing at all. A test compares the generator against an untouched twin for exactly that.

The egg's numbers and the embryo's are different numbers

The egg gets quality 4 and quantity 100; the serpent inside it quality 8 and a quantity rolled from 4 to 8. These are not the same pair twice: the egg's quality is an alignment and its quantity a hatching percentage, while the embryo's quality is an AI mode and its quantity the number of serpents. In the original they are one 16-bit word, which is exactly the shape that makes them easy to confuse.

Reachability measured, not assumed

The shipped world holds exactly one silver horn, on level 5 at (72, 193), and its weight byte is nought, so it is used where it lies. The file also holds 18 silver serpents as objects, but all of them are embryos inside eggs, and they cannot trigger the refusal, which scans actor slots rather than objects.

And the same question for the previous slice: where does a carried telescope scroll from?

The telescope arm reads the object's own square, and a carried object has none. That was left open in an old brief as the question "which arms read the target's square after the object is found", and the brief called it the slice's real content. The list is one arm long: target.X and target.Y appear once in the whole 13,000-line view, and it is the telescope.

What a carried object's column is: its owner's number, both in the original, where those two bytes hold the owner, and in this project's export, which keeps the same bits. Measured over the shipped objects: every carried object's exported column equals its owner.

But it is not reachable through the get command, and that measurement refuted my own worry rather than confirming it: the telescope's weight byte is nought, and a weight of nought means CannotBeTaken, not light. The thing cannot be picked up. The move command and pickpocket were not checked, so the comment says "not through get" and not "never", and nothing went into the bug list, because reachability cannot be shown.

Next
