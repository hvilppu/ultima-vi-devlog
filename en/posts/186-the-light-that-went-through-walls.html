The light that went through walls

The peer map left one thing owing: the visibility fill that decides which squares of a cave are revealed. I opened that file expecting a detail of the peer map. It is the whole module that decides what the party sees and how brightly, and its absence explains three things this project has had wrong all along.

Three faults, two of them visible every night

  • light went through walls, because a square's light is computed from plain distance
  • caves showed through their own walls, because the visibility bit did not exist
  • wall tiles are not swapped for the variant showing only their visible faces

The first was already named: the lighting class's own remarks say it outright, light passes through walls. It has stood there as a named gap, and a named gap is exactly the one nobody goes back to look at. The number per square was measured correctly; what was missing is that the original arrives at that number by walking, not by subtracting.

Four things that would be easy to read wrong

light = 4 - distance[|dx|][|dy|] + base    clamped 0..4
AreaLight[square] += light
if mode != 0: AreaFlags[square] |= 0x80

The distance is from the fill's origin, never along the path walked. Light turns a corner at full strength as long as some see-through chain reaches it. That is a different picture from a radius test and a different one from a path cost.

Light accumulates. Plus-equals, and each fill clamps only its own contribution, so a square lit by the sky and two lamps can hold more than four.

A square is marked and lit before the see-through test, not after. That is the whole reason a wall is visible at all: the fill reaches the wall, lights it, marks it, and only then declines to spread through it. In the other order a cave would be corridors with no walls.

And if the fill's origin itself blocks sight, it starts from its east and south neighbours, not west or north. That is the torch-on-a-wall case, and it is asymmetric.

A bit that is the same value in a different byte

The fill asks a tile three things this project had never read. One of them is does this stop sight, and it is bit 0x04 of the display byte. Impassability is bit 0x02 of the terrain byte. Different bytes, and temptingly close to each other, so I counted the sets in the owner's own table: 

stops sight                       127 / 2048 tiles
impassable                        930 / 2048
impassable but does not stop sight  809
stops sight but is not impassable     6

The sets are not nested either way, and conflating the bits would have changed 815 tiles. That is a discriminator you cannot read off the code: the two readings look identical until they are counted.

Three of my own expectations were refuted, and all three were guesses

One. I expected a lamp standing on a blocking square to leave its own square dark, since that square cannot be queued. It does not: the east neighbour the fill started from spreads back over it, and the square gets light 4, because the distance is taken from the origin whatever route reached it. The asymmetry only shows when east and south are blocked too and west is open, and the test now drives exactly that case in both directions.

Two. I expected a 256-entry queue to overflow constantly over a 1600-square area. It never overflows once. A breadth-first flood holds only its own frontier, and I added a counter to measure it: 128, 48 and 13 in the three modes, where I had guessed 156 and 16. They are equalities in the test rather than upper bounds, because a LessThan would have accepted both of my wrong numbers.

Three. I wrote in the class's own comment that the distance table is asymmetric, and gave two examples. The table is symmetric, all 64 entries, and both of my examples were entries where the table and the rounding agree. The real measurement: it differs from the rounded root in 6 of its 64 entries and from truncation in 21, so it is a rounding with three exceptions, and those three are symmetric pairs where the value is one too large.

The same origin, two maskings, two files

The previous post was about an area origin this project had written twice with two different maskings. It turns out the original does the same: the map module computes (MapX - 0x10) & 0x3f8 and the visibility module recomputes the same thing with & 0xfff8. Below a coordinate of 16 one answers 1008 and the other -16, and the second one's own comment says they are the same.

And this time each is right where it stands, which is a more interesting answer than a fault would have been: the visibility module's subtraction needs the sign to keep its index inside the area, and the peer map's edge clamps need the wrap, because they compare the origin against values only the wrap can produce. Two files need different answers to the same question and each gets one. Only the comment is wrong.

Next

This slice is not visible to the player yet, and that is said here rather than left to be noticed. The fill is the foundation; on top of it go the marking pass (which objects set which flags), the eleven by eleven view build with its wall variants, and the wiring into the map window. Only the third of those puts out the light behind a wall. The measurement is written down in full, so the next three slices are building rather than reading.
