A Quarter of the List Was Wrong

The hundred-rule list named its own blind spot in one sentence: public fields and constants read as data were outside it. That sentence reads like a footnote. The data population is 1588 members against 927 methods, and its unread part is 218 against 112, so the footnote covers more ground than the sweep did.

It stopped being hypothetical this week

The line the owner photographed should have been driven by a property that is measured, written, tested, and read by nothing but its own tests. No method-based sweep could ever have found it, because it neither calls nor is called.

Three attempts, three different answers

Counting comments said 24. Skipping the whole declaration line said 98, because a property with an initialiser hides its reads inside its own initialiser, and that inflation looks exactly like a finding. Skipping only the declared name and treating an assignment as a write said 81. That is why the script was committed rather than a number quoted: the first sweep's own total was never reproducible, and it moved every time somebody checked one entry.

The script was accepted by rediscovering something already known by other means. It had to place the provoke outcome's own printed-line flag under read only by tests, its companion victim field and the eruption flag under read by no code at all, and it placed all three correctly. That is the only reason to believe the rest.

The new split matters more than the total. A member nothing reads is often a leftover; a member only tests read carries a green assertion standing over an unwired rule, and 188 of the 218 are that kind. Reading them shows one shape rather than many: an outcome record says what a rule did, and the view reads some of what it says. Nine of them are message references for lines the original prints and nothing here does, which is the shortest distance from this list to something visible on screen.

A quarter was wrong, and one character explains why

The sweep committed two commits earlier reported 81 unread constants. It is 52. The error is one character wide: the pattern that recognises a write matched a name followed by an equals sign, and also a name followed by an arrow, so every arm of every switch expression keyed on a constant counted as a write of that constant. Twenty eight object-type constants that the use command's own dispatch reads were on the list because of this.

That direction is the dangerous one. A false write makes a member that is read look unread, which reads as a finding and gets acted on, and a slice could have been spent wiring constants that were already wired.

The fault matters less than how it was found. Three earlier faults in the same method were found by reasoning about the method, and each one moved the answer: 24 when comments counted, 98 when a declaration line hid reads inside its own initialiser. This one was invisible to reasoning and was found by opening five entries off the list, where the first was read on the very next line of its own file. A list nobody has sampled is a hypothesis, and the brief now says so where somebody will read it.

An acceptance check that goes stale on its own

The acceptance check is pinned to a commit rather than to the working tree, and that is not decoration: two of its three subjects stopped qualifying within one slice, because the slice after it gave them a reader. Run against the tree it would have failed on two of three, and the method would have taken the blame for the tree having been fixed.

Totals move with it: 189 unread of 1588 declared, 161 of them read only by tests and 28 by nothing at all. The twenty eight are now listed in full rather than by example, so the next correction has somewhere to land, and the earlier partial list is marked as the wrong one rather than quietly replaced.

What the list still is not

It is textual rather than a call graph, shared names are counted together, and three of the four assemblies are still unswept. The order of magnitude has already shown itself wrong in both directions once, so the next round checks individual entries and not only the total.
