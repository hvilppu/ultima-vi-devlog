An egg competing with the whole continent

The test harness from the last post found its first catch: egg hatching sweeps the whole level every time, not the original's own window bounded to the 40 by 40 area around the party. This was recorded as a harmless performance choice when eggs were first wired to hatch. It was not harmless: one sweep filled all thirty two temporary actor slots, every time, and an egg beside the party competed for a slot with every egg on the continent.

The right question, the wrong conclusion

The original reasoning sounded sensible: the original's own loaded window never changes which egg hatches, only when it is first considered, so sweeping the whole level could only hatch an egg sooner than the original, never differently. That is true looked at one egg at a time. It never asked how many eggs compete for the same thirty two slots in the same sweep, when the candidates number three hundred for the level instead of a handful near the party.

Three consequences followed, and the third is the one that shows up in play: which thirty two eggs won a slot depended on iteration order, not on where the party stood; most of what hatched was far away and the purge deleted it almost immediately, so the work went to creatures nobody would ever meet; and an egg beside the party competed for slots with every egg on the continent, so the player saw fewer creatures than the original would give them, and which ones was effectively arbitrary.

The fix: only the extent changed

The egg's own eight tile distance gate, the day and night gates, the one shot roll and the local exception are all untouched. The sweep now walks only the party's own 40 by 40 area, aligned with the identical formula this project's own area-change gate and the purge's own centre already used. The eight tile gate and the area bound compose: an egg is a candidate only when it sits inside the area and is further than eight tiles away (or local, or hatching is forced). A new lookup, U6ObjectWorld.AreaObjects, walks this project's own by-square index instead of scanning every record: an egg outside the area is never read at all, not merely filtered after being read.

Measured: the pool, before and after

Before: one sweep fills all 32 slots, every time, with or without the purge. After: in the owner's own exported data, the busiest single aligned 40 by 40 area on level 0 holds seventeen eggs, nowhere near thirty two, so one sweep can no longer fill the pool by itself. Scanning the whole level without the purge, each of its 676 aligned windows visited exactly once, exhausts the pool at window 110 and stays exhausted for the rest of the scan; the same scan with the purge places 316 actors across the whole level and the pool never once runs dry in between.

Measured: the sweep's own cost

A new counter, U6ObjectWorld.AreaSquaresVisited, built the same way as the last post's own ChainRecordsVisited: before the fix the sweep's cost was the world's own size, 16838 records in the owner's data, every single sweep. After the fix it costs exactly 1600 square lookups, forty by forty, regardless of world size: measured identical on both a three object fixture and the owner's own 16838 object world.

Two numbers nobody had counted

How many eggs a level holds, and how many fit in one area: level 0 carries 301 eggs, and averages 0.46 eggs per aligned 40 by 40 area. Thirty two slots is therefore a generous number in the original's own terms: no real area measured comes close to it alone, and the average area carries less than one egg. That is the number that answers whether F6's own original choice was reasonable or not, measured rather than guessed.
