Three Nearly Identical Rectangles

The screen picture is whole, so the next layer is the one that carries a click into a command. I wrote a brief for it, and the brief had two errors. Both were mine, and one of them was a number I had myself written down as a different thing.

The rule is four zones and their order

The original walks its hit zones and stops at the first one containing the pointer: the map, the icon strip, the panel, the whole screen.

The last contains all the others. That is why the walk needs no bound, and why the order is the entire mechanism: a point inside the map resolves to the map only because the map is tried before the whole screen. The discriminator is therefore free, since a different order gives a different answer for almost every point on the screen. When the build broke it on purpose, three of the four zones answered wrongly at once.

And then the trap

Three nearly identical rectangles cover the same area on the right of the screen: 

the panel's fill     168, 6, 312, 103
the mouse-off call   176, 6, 311, 103
the hit zone         168, 8, 311, 103

They differ in both their left edges and both their tops, and no two of them are the same measurement. I wrote the hit zone's top into the brief as 6, which is the middle row's number in the wrong place.

The uncomfortable part: this project's own layout document records those as distinct, and I wrote that document. Writing something down does not stop you confusing it later.

The build read the table itself, used 8, and said plainly that the two were never the same measurement. The warning is in the document now, put there precisely because the person who wrote the document walked into the trap anyway.

The second error was in a discriminator

I asked the whole screen zone for "no command". It answers the pass command and resets the selection. So my discriminator was wrong about the original rather than a deliberate simplification.

The build built to the brief's wording and flagged the difference, which is the right process: a brief is an instruction, not evidence. I corrected the half that can be corrected, the command, and the selection reset stays unbuilt because it needs the state the map zone's own exclusion keeps out.

Two lines of real behaviour

On the icon strip, the left button runs the command and the right button arms it as the default. A right click on the map then performs whatever is armed. Both answers are asserted from one position, because either alone would pass with the two paths swapped.

And it is deliberately dead

Nothing in this project's view polls a pointer. So the rule is complete, tested and called by nobody.

That is a named state here, and I asked for it to be said plainly rather than for a caller to be wired in passing. Wiring one would need a poll of pointer and buttons once a frame, an edge detector for the press, and the panel's current view fed in. None of that was asked for.
