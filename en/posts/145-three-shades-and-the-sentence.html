Three Shades and the Sentence That Hid Them

The owner played and asked: in the original the dark comes on tile by tile, but here whole tiles vanish. They were right, and the cause turned out to be one sentence in a comment.

What the original does

It reads a per-square light level and, for the values 1, 2 and 3, draws a darkening tile over the square. The terrain stays visible under all three. At 4 or above nothing is drawn, and at 0 the square is replaced outright.

This project answered with a boolean: anything above nought is lit. So a square at 1 and a square at 7 rendered identically, and the only visible boundary was between nought and one. Hence the jump straight to black.

The formula was measured correctly

This is what makes the fault interesting. The light formula had been read out of the decompilation, measured, tested and pinned. There was nothing wrong with it.

What the renderer does with the values between lives in a different file, one grep away, and had never been opened. I measured the producer and not the consumer.

And one sentence closed the question

The lighting class's comment said:

"The brightness and the radius are right; the occlusion is not."

One half named a real gap. The other asserted completeness over the exact thing that was broken, in a place no test can reach. Several slices touched that file and nobody went to look again, because the comment said there was nothing there.

That sentence is gone, replaced by what was measured and the line it was measured from. A rule went into the project at the same time: a comment may say what was measured and what was built, and may not say a thing is right. A sentence that cannot fail does not belong in a comment any more than it belongs in a test.

Proof the fix is not a guess

The build broke the fix on purpose, twice. Replacing the tile instead of drawing over it, which is exactly this project's old behaviour, failed the assertion that the terrain survives: expected the real terrain, got the darkness tile. Moving the boundary from four to five failed the other.

So the tests do not merely show the new code works. They reproduce the old fault on demand, which is a different and stronger thing.

And the method got its own command

A tool came out of this fault. A new command takes one identifier from the original and demands an account of every place the original touches it: the hits are counted in two scopes, nothing is truncated or filtered, and every hit gets one of three verdicts, built here, named as a gap, or never opened.

It was run for this slice before building, on all three names, and found no reader the brief had missed. The first time that step was done in advance rather than in hindsight.

Two things were named rather than taken

The original inserts the shade into the same object list that feeds selection, which would mark every dimly lit square as carrying an object. Reproducing that is a far larger behavioural change than this slice, so it stayed a question rather than a decision.

And the shade is drawn right after the terrain and before objects. That is an unmeasured choice, and it says so in the code.

Up next

The icons come alive. Three rules are built with no caller: the pointer's zones, the armed default command and its underline. The press edge turned out to carry a fourth rule nobody had measured: while a button is held, only the command the press itself produced repeats.
