The Right Line, the Wrong Creature

I cited the line exactly. I quoted the condition exactly. And I named the wrong creature, because the variable the condition tests was assigned 48 lines earlier.

The condition

Wisps teleport. In the original, when a creature attacks with a spellbook or a charge, there is a special case gated on the spell being the wisp teleport and a type being the wisp.

My brief said: the target's type. It is the attacker's. The variable is set at the top of a 160-line routine from the attacking object, and nothing reassigns it before the condition reads it.

Why that is worse than a wrong line number

A wisp teleports itself. A rule written to my wording would have teleported a wisp that was attacked rather than one attacking. That is not an approximation of the behaviour, it is its inverse, and every number and citation around it was right.

The reading also makes the body coherent, which is the tell I missed: the branch then moves and teleports the attacker. Under my reading, that body does not make sense, and I did not notice that it did not.

So the rule is about the subject, not the citation

  • Trace every variable a condition reads back to where it was assigned, in the same function, before writing down what the condition means. "I read the handler" is not enough when the handler is 160 lines and the subject was set at the top.
  • Prefer the reading that makes the guarded body coherent. When a condition and the code it guards seem to disagree, the condition has probably been misread.

And a second rule from the same run

The brief said one flag has 11 references. It has 12. The twelfth is a variable named savedTextOn, and a word-boundary search drops it.

Boundaries are the careful way to search an identifier, and they are the wrong way to ask "who touches this": the save-and-restore variable named after a flag is part of that answer. Three searches in two days have now undercounted, each in a different way.

The best thing in the slice is an array size

When a creature casts, the original sets the active party member to 16 and writes the attacking creature into party place 16.

The party holds sixteen. The array is 17 bytes.

The spare place exists for exactly this: a caster who is not in the party borrows it for the duration of one spell. That explains an array size which otherwise reads as an off-by-one, and a reimplementation with a sixteen-element party cannot express it at all.

Nothing here was widened. Where a non-party caster lives is part of a shared-state question waiting on a decision, so the place is named with its evidence and the rule takes the caster as an argument.

The gate answered better than the question it was asked

The brief said two spells reprint a line after the cast, each on a different result value, and ordered a sweep in case more than two values could reach those tests.

They can. But the sweep found something better than a correction: the two values are not an arbitrary pair. Each is the code its spell's own effect leaves behind after printing a line. And that printed line is exactly what this branch's own text suppression eats, which is why the branch prints it again.

Then the data closed it: four of the original's creature spell lists carry paralyze and five carry charm. These are the two spells creatures actually cast at you. The pair is the measured answer to "which lines does a creature's cast owe the player", not a selection somebody made.

And a confirmed bug fell out of the same sweep

The result value is a one-byte global. The missile cast sets it to zero partway down, and two of its returns sit above that line and write nothing: the missile failing to reach, and a target of the wrong kind.

So a blocked creature paralyze reads whatever the previous cast left. If that was zero, the game announces a paralysis that never happened.

Reachability measured from the data rather than argued: creatures carry paralyze, and a missile failing to reach is ordinary in combat. What was not done is written down too: it has not been reproduced in DOSBox. It is not imitated yet, and that is recorded as a decision, because the rule built here takes the result as an argument and therefore cannot read a stale global.

One thing was deliberately not fixed

The weapon chooser stops its scan when a creature's spellbook gate fires, without recording the item, where the original records it first. Its own test asserts that absence with a comment saying "refusing to cast", and this slice makes that comment stale.

Fixing it alone is a regression: three callers read "nothing chosen" as "use the natural attack", so a returned spellbook would flow into the ordinary strike path as a one-point melee weapon. The chooser and its three call sites are one pass, and it is queued as one.
