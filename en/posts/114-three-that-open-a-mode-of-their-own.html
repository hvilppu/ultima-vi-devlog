Three that open a mode of their own

Enchant, gate travel and wizard eye were the last three spells in the whole game that had never been measured. With them, every one of the eighty spells now has a measured handler, for the first time in this project's history. That number is worth saying carefully, because seven of them still cannot be cast and reach nothing in play.

A guard that can never fire

Enchant writes a charge of another spell into a staff. It picks a target and runs straight into a guard that is supposed to reject anything but a carried object: the selection is absent, and the selection is an object, and the selection is not lying on the map. The first two conditions cannot both hold at once, an absent selection cannot also be an object, so the whole guard is dead code. The decompilation's own comment guesses the author meant "or" instead of "and".

The result: the only test that ever runs is the type check after it, which demands the target be a staff. The spell therefore enchants any staff whatsoever, whether it is lying on the ground or sitting in somebody's pack, when the clear intent was to stop exactly the ground case. Recorded in originalBugs.md as a confirmed anomaly and imitated as measured: this project never reads whether the object is held at all at this point, precisely because the original's guard never reaches that check.

The same spell hides a second exact bound: a staff's own contents refuse at exactly ten items, not ten or more. A staff that already holds eleven of anything sails straight through a gate that was meant to stop it at ten.

A refusal you cannot hear without already knowing

Gate travel asks for a typed number and teleports the party to that moonstone's own position. Every one of its five refusal conditions, a bad character, an empty line, zero, more than eight, or an unplaced moonstone, does the exact same thing: plays a sound and prints nothing at all. It is the only refusal in the entire spell file that is silent in text and audible in sound. A player who does not recognize that chime gets no other clue why the trip never started.

This spell also named its own gap: this project has no way to move the whole party at once, only a single character. The handler therefore reports where the party would arrive and moves nobody, honestly, because inventing a stand in for the party's own regrouping would have been a guess at exactly the point where measurement has nothing to say.

Forty, which turns out to be forty one

Wizard eye opens a roaming eye the player steers with directions, seeing through walls wherever it goes, closing once its moves run out. The decompilation's own comment says the move count is forty. The code says otherwise: the counter starts at forty and the loop keeps going while it is zero or more, so the move taken when the counter reads zero is already the forty first, and only the decrement after that one takes it negative.

This is not a bug in the original game, and it is not recorded in originalBugs.md as one: the game itself behaves exactly the same way on every single cast, forty one moves, whether or not anyone ever read that comment. The mismatch sits between the comment and the code, not in the original's own behavior, and it is exactly the shape this project has been caught by before: a prose number sitting next to a bound whose own code disagrees, where the prose is the thing a port copies without testing it. Forty one is now measured and asserted as its own test, and the 42nd move is refused in that same test.

One smaller note from the same loop: a key that is not a direction spends no move at all, it is simply read and ignored. A player who presses anything but a direction can hold the eye open for as long as they like.

Eighty, said precisely

With these three, every one of the eighty spells in the game now has a measured handler: 61 through their shared family, 12 through the ninth delegate, and these final three as their own classes. That is a line worth saying, and worth saying correctly.

It does not mean all eighty are reachable by a player. 73 actually reach their handler in play, and 7 reach nothing at all, each one waiting on one of four named missing interaction modes: a direction prompt and the shared "last direction given" value it writes (wind change, telekinesis' push, wizard eye's own loop), a typed line of text (gate travel), a pick from another character's pack (pickpocket's second selection), and a pick from the spellbook page (enchant's second prompt). Not one of these is an unmeasured rule. All of them are measured, built handlers waiting on a keyboard this game has not yet had any way to offer anyone.
