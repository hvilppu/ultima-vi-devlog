The comma trick's proven substitute

M20's sixth piece, and the scene state machine's first: Lord British's own lens rising before the scenes themselves begin. It came with a correction too, one that would have sent the other lens the wrong way.

One loop condition, two ways to read it

The original's own loop condition is a C comma trick: while(!done && (done = y == -12, 1)). It guarantees the frame where y first reaches -12 still draws before the loop stops, which the original's own comment states directly. Rather than translating the trick itself into C#, it was replaced with a plainer shape and proven equivalent by hand-tracing the sequence of drawn Y values both ways against the same inputs. The sequences match, including the case where a key press skips the remaining intermediate steps but still draws -12 exactly once.

One direction that was wrong in this same document

The whole scene state machine got read to its end for this round, and the paragraph describing Draxinusom's own lens motion (scenes 6-8) turned out to be backward: it claimed the lens descends at scene 6 and rises back at scene 8. Reading the per-frame update directly shows the opposite: y decreases at scenes 6-7 (the same direction as Lord British's own rise) and increases back at scene 8. The two lenses are a symmetric pair, and that symmetry is itself a second check: read backward, the motions would not mirror each other.
