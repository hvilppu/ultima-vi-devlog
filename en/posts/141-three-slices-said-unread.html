Three Slices Said It Was Unread

Every time a part of the screen stayed black, I wrote the same sentence: "the border art is a loaded picture nobody has read". Three slices in a row ended on it. Reading it took one session.

The mechanism is three lines

The original opens the first of five named picture files, decompresses it, and draws it as a pixmap at the origin. The drawing driver's own comment gives the layout outright: a width word, a height word, then the rows, with no clipping.

And it is called exactly once, at startup, before the command icons and before anything else. That is the whole explanation for why the original's margins carry an ornate border and ours were black: everything is drawn over it.

Three files, one structure, three sizes

main screen background   3881 on disk   decompresses to 64004  =  4 + 320 x 200
world map picture        3500 on disk   decompresses to 16388  =  4 + 128 x 128
small scroll              998 on disk   decompresses to 11812  =  4 + 144 x  82

This is the whole point of the discriminator. One file cannot tell a four byte header from a four byte trailer or from no header at all, because a single number fits several explanations. Three files at three sizes can, and not one of the numbers was chosen to fit.

The discriminator that actually earns its keep

A background is easy to test wrongly. Load the file, check the length, confirm it decompressed: all of that passes, and the picture can still end up entirely underneath the other draws and invisible. That kind of test proves the code ran, not that it did anything.

So two of the assertions are about where the background ends up. A pixel inside the map window must carry the map's value after a full compose. A pixel outside every other step's rectangle must carry the background's own. Both were broken on purpose, the first by composing the background last and the second by not composing it at all, and both failed.

One measured number, not a range that passes

The decompressed screen contains 18 distinct palette indices. That number was measured first and pinned into the test as it stands, not bounded: a failed decompression gives either one index or very nearly 256, and both are a long way from eighteen.

And the values say something else. They are {0, 20-22, 49-62}, so the whole border is painted in the paper colour's own family of shades, and 49 is exactly the paper colour that turned up in a variable initialiser a couple of posts ago. The same number from a different place, and they agree.

One note worth keeping

The composition order in this project is a value rather than a call order, but most of that order is recorded from what the view happened to do. The background is different: the original measures it first.

That is now said in the list's own remarks. Measured and recorded look identical in code, and the difference is worth writing down on the occasions when it is known.
