The armour nobody added up

M14's slice E3: a rat walks up to the party, decides to attack, and the previous slice spent its movement points and nothing else. This slice applies the hit itself: a miss, a graze, damage, death, in exactly the order the original runs them. Six branches were named instead of built, and one of the brief's own claims turned out wrong for one input out of six, not zero.

Five out of six, not six

The brief claimed every input a hit needs, the defender's dexterity, the attacker's strength or dexterity, the weapon's damage, protection and undead status, already had a built primitive. Five did. The sixth did not: U6Combat.Protection takes an already summed armour total in, it does not sum one itself, and nothing else summed a defender's own worn armour before this slice. Without it, every single hit on a party member would have read protection as zero, whatever the character actually wears. Built as U6AttackRules.EquippedProtection, sitting next to the existing EquippedWeapons, with the same shape: walk the equipped items, add up their armour values.

A provoke check nobody has ever built

The original runs the neutral provoke check right after the graze and the damage, and on a killing blow exactly the same way as on a survivor: the only gate is whether the defender was the deliberately chosen target, not whether the defender lived. An earlier draft of this report claimed otherwise, read from the decompilation's own summary document rather than the handler itself; the handler is the authority and the summary was wrong, now corrected and pinned by a test. The check itself this engine has never built, not in this slice and not in the two earlier spell slices that already report their own ProvokeAmount with nobody reading it back. It needs a guard alert radius, scheduled NPC tracking and a notion of a temporary monster, three things that do not exist, and the gap grows with every slice that adds another amount nobody reads. U6HitApplication keeps the same shape: it reports the amount and does nothing with it.

Four flags, not one, and they were blocking three rules

The skeleton's own Traits field in monsters.json decoded cleanly into names. The rat's own decoded as a raw number, 10308. An earlier draft of this report found one missing flag and stopped there. The real count, read from the owner's own decompilation, is four: above the existing Ethereal flag sit NoCorpse, Vanishes, Bleeds and Human. The discriminator that settles it this time: OR every one of the 64 classes' own flags together and the result is exactly the mask of all fifteen named flags, with not one class carrying a bit outside it.

This was not only a documentation gap: it was blocking three measured rules in the death branch, which the original runs through the same code as falling asleep. A type found in the corpse table that does not vanish takes its corpse shape; otherwise, on death, a gazer would spawn insects, a no-corpse or vanishing class would be deleted outright, a slime would refresh its frames; and a bleeding class leaves a pool of blood, but only after the object is actually added, checked as its own separate step. The rat and the skeleton show the two opposite ends of this: the rat never gets a corpse and always bleeds, the skeleton always gets a corpse and never bleeds. Four flags added, the export regenerated, and the death branch built on top of them in this same slice, because the gap was blocking code that was being written right then.
