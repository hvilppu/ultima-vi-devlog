Thirty-Six of Forty-Five

M14's slice A was left open on one half: what happens to the shot itself once it has already flown, COMBAT_FireRangedWeapon. It was waiting on the line of fire slice C0 built, and this piece closes that half for good: ammunition spent, a thrown weapon's fate, burning oil, Zu Ylem's sleep, a wand's vanishing.

A puddle is not deep water

The natural reading for a thrown weapon is "it is lost in the water". Measured from the owner's own tileflags.bin, 45 of 2048 tiles are wet, but only 9 of those are also impassable, which is deep water. A thrown weapon, a spear, a throwing axe or a dagger, is only destroyed on those nine plus four void tiles, thirteen in all. Thirty-six of the forty-five wet tiles are a puddle, where the weapon drops to the bottom instead of vanishing. And even a deep, empty tile is not quite enough on its own: the condition also requires that nothing already stands on the square. A raft or a bridge piece over deep water drops the weapon onto it instead of swallowing it.

The oil reads the same byte, a different bit

The flask of oil's rule reads exactly the same terrain byte as the thrown weapon's rule, but only its wet bit. A fire field fizzles on all 45 wet tiles, puddles and deep water alike, even where a dagger would drop all the way to the bottom on 36 of them. On the very same deep water tile, the dagger is destroyed and the oil still only fizzles, not one rule folded into the other but two separate bits of the same byte.

Two counts inside one triple crossbow

The triple crossbow fires up to three bolts on one shot, and it has two different ways of counting bolts inside the same action. How many fly is TestObj's recursive total over the whole inventory, capped at three. How many are spent falls on FindInvType's own first stack alone, and that stack is deleted entirely when it holds no more than three. A single stack never disagrees between the two, so the difference never shows in ordinary play. But two stacks, two and two bolts, fire three and pay two: the third bolt comes free. A port that computed both counts the same way would get the single stack case right and every split stack wrong, always in the direction of charging more than the original does.

A hit before terrain, never both

When a thrown weapon hits an actor, it is destroyed, and terrain is never consulted after that at all. The two branches are strict and mutually exclusive: "it fell in the water after hitting him" is not a case the original recognises. Zu Ylem splits the same way but in the other direction: it is always spent regardless of the hit, and only a hit would open its own sleep rule, which this project refuses to cast yet, by name, the same shape of refusal the player's own Attack command and the basic AI's weapon choice already use. A lightning or fire wand does not care about the hit at all: it is destroyed on exactly one chance in a hundred on every shot, hit or not.
