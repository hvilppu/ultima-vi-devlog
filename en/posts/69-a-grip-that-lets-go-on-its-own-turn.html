A grip that lets go on its own turn

M14's slice E2: combat now runs through the same turn loop the previous slice gave walking. Nine measured combat modes that have existed and been tested for months got their first caller. Wiring it up found two ways this very brief's own count of nine was wrong, and one piece of game mechanics that was not where it was assumed to be.

A grip that lets go on its own turn

In melee, the Leader field remembers the last attacker standing next to a character, and while it is set a ranged shot refuses to fire: the grip holds. The question is when it lets go. The original resets it in two places, both under the same condition (NPCMode between 2 and 15): at the top of the turn loop, for the controlled actor, and at the end of every actor's own turn, for itself. The second is the one that settles it: the grip lets go on the engaged actor's own turn, not the instant it is hit and not only once something else happens to clear it.

That is not a value to pin, it is two turns. The test hits an actor with an adjacent attack, checks that a ranged shot refuses while that stands, runs the actor's own turn, and checks that it no longer refuses. A port that left Leader set until something else cleared it would produce a creature that can never fire again once anyone has closed on it; this is what pins that the engine does not do that.

Ten entry points, not nine

Wiring this up meant reading NPC_TakeTurn's own switch exactly, not as the brief's prose but straight from the decompilation. It is ten distinct function calls, not the nine the brief counted: two pairs share one entry point each (AI_ASSAULT/AI_VIGILANTE, and AI_RETREAT/AI_SHY/AI_FEAR/AI_SCHED_SHY), and counting each shared entry once still leaves ten, not nine. The number was corrected rather than forced to agree.

Two conditions that look like the same thing twice and are not

The numeric range (2 to 15) that resets Leader and the dispatch switch's own set of modes look, on a first read, like the same fact written down twice. They are not: AI_FEAR (19), AI_SCHED_SHY (154) and AI_VIGILANTE (155) all dispatch to a combat handler but sit numerically outside the range, so their own Leader never resets by it. Conversely AI_GRAZE (12) and the east-west patrol (15) sit inside the range but dispatch to no combat handler at all. A port that used one test for both would reset Leader for a grazing cow and leave it unreset for a creature that was just hit and fought back: wrong in both directions at once.

Five delegates nobody had ever assembled from the real game

The combat modes' own entry points take five inputs (can the actor see the target, can it step onto a square, does a square block a shot) that no earlier slice had ever assembled from anything but a test's own fakes. Three were assembled straight from already measured pieces as they stand. Two had to be named as gaps: canSee here only tests the same level, not invisibility and not a buried tangle pod, because neither is modelled in this project's character data; and the step's own terrain cost in combat borrows the walking formula as is, because the original's own formula was never measured for a combat caller, its own comments say so directly. A tangle pod's own new vine segment always refuses to be created, by name: the pod can still move and attack, it just never leaves a vine behind it, because this engine has no primitive yet for adding a new object to the map.
