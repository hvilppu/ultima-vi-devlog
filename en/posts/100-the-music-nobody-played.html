The music nobody played

The songs were exported, the command stream measured command by command, the parser, the repeat stack and the tick clock built and tested. The game made no sound. The songs are not notes, they are OPL2 register writes, and there was no chip; nor did the area music choice have a single caller. The whole subsystem was finished up to the point where it would have become audible.

Thirteen steps and not one sound

This is the most frequent fault in this project, and this time it took a whole subsystem rather than one rule. Every piece was right: the format read out of the driver's own rewrite, fourteen commands, a sixteen level repeat stack with its own misparse, a tick rate of 60.0011 hertz and not a round sixty. All of it pinned by tests. And none of those tests could notice that at the end of the chain there was nothing to turn a register write into sound.

The owner found it in one sentence: in the real game a melody plays in the background, here only the effects.

One bit of silence

The chip is nine channels, two operators each, envelopes and four waveforms. It was finished and produced exactly nought. The fault was the register group mask: the channel registers are four bits of group and four of channel, the operator ones three and five, and I had folded both into one three bit mask. 

0xA0 & 0xE0 = 0xA0
0xB0 & 0xE0 = 0xA0

B0 is the register the key on bit lives in. Every note landed on the frequency's low byte. The chip worked perfectly and no note ever started, and that mistake is now a test of its own.

Three tests were wrong and the code was right

Three of the driver's tests failed, and each time tracing it back to the original showed my expectation had been a guess. A wait of three takes four ticks, because the test is --WAIT == 0. A volume slide's first step lands in the same tick the command was read in. And a wait of nought still ends the tick, it simply does not count down. I fixed the tests and not the code, which is the right direction only when the original has been read first.

The same week, the same shape, a third time

Before the music the same week gave two more of these. A gargoyle started on the Avatar's own square: the rule that an actor blocks its square was built and thorough, and the egg hatch never called it. The reason was visible in the signature, the original's predicate is CanMoveTo(objNum, x, y) and ours was (x, y), and that dropped first argument was exactly the actor half.

And the text window behaved differently in conversation than elsewhere. The original has one console and three windows; we have two code paths, one of which handles all twelve escape characters and the other none.

What measuring saved

The web build did not start at all, and the fix first looked like rewriting 42 read sites. Two measurements cut it to a fraction: every read in the view goes through one function, and the browser's persistent path is a real file system even though the streaming folder is not. One copy at startup, and the rest of the chain works unchanged, saving included.
