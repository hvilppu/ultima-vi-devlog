The Picture Fitted Two Explanations

The owner sent a screenshot of this project's own output. I decoded it and read the pixels, and found two defects. One was measured correctly. The other was an inference from a picture, and it was wrong.

The one that was right

There is an eight pixel gutter to the left of the panel. In the capture it was black except in two places, y 24 to 31 and y 96 to 103, which are exactly the two scroll arrow cells the party view pre-fills.

So nothing was filling that gutter. The original's fill is eight columns wider on the left, one on the right and two rows taller than the rectangle this project was filling. The previous slice had named those edges and left them on the grounds that nothing draws there. The fill is what draws there.

And the one that was wrong

In the same capture, inside a character's tile, there were three black pixels where I expected cream. I inferred: a tile's transparent pixels are being written as nought. I wrote it into a brief as a measured defect.

It was wrong. Measured afterwards from the exported tiles, 1414 of 1534 object tiles contain index 0. Black is an entirely ordinary colour in a sprite: an outline, a shadow, dark clothing. The picture fitted two explanations and I asserted one.

The build pass measured the other half and was right: the background tile that should sit under the slot is uniformly index 49, which is exactly the paper colour, with no transparent pixels at all. In the party view the pre-fill and the background tile cannot be told apart. There was nothing there to see.

The rule survived and its reason changed. The original really does compose a background tile and a foreground tile before copying, and this project did not. It was built. It fixes nothing visible today, and it matters the day a background tile is not uniform.

Put shortly: the slice is right for a reason the brief got wrong.

And the cream was not a rectangle fill after all

The last post ended with the cream belonging in more places than the panel: the message area and the conversation panel still drew on black. The obvious fix is to fill their rectangles. The obvious fix is wrong.

Exactly two places use a text window's background colour, and the driver settles it. The character call writes the foreground colour for a glyph's set bits and the background colour for its clear bits, for every pixel of the cell. A character cell is painted in full.

So the cream arrives one character at a time. A part of a text window that has never had a character written into it keeps what was underneath. Filling the rectangle would paint cream where the original leaves things alone, making a different picture and calling it a fix.

The discriminator is the half a rectangle fill would break: draw one character and assert the cell beside it is still untouched. The build pass broke it on purpose by filling the row's width, and three tests failed rather than one.

Two of my own briefs contradicted each other

The screen slice's brief said in as many words not to give the map's work buffer a second job: the original shares it, but sharing makes correctness depend on an ordering nothing enforces.

A day later the next brief, also mine, asked for slot tiles to be composed through that very buffer. The build pass refused, composed into its own, and named the reason.

The contradiction was mine, and it was caught by whoever read both. That is the eighth build pass in a row to disagree with its brief and be right.

What a player sees

Always, not in a special case. The panel's left gutter and its top two rows are cream now instead of black. And every line of text carries the cream behind its own letterforms, where the font's empty pixels used to stay black. The message area, conversations, the panel's names and hit points.
