Seven things you can press

Four measured decisions were waiting on the owner, and he asked for them to be made here instead. After them, seven things landed that a player can press: saving, the sextant, digging, the staff, the orb of the moons, the horse and a book's text. Seventy-eight of the eighty-five usable types now answer.

Saving wrote the world, and first it nearly destroyed the world's own source

The whole save path was built and tested and the game could not call it: the classes sat on the tool side, which Unity cannot see. The move was three files.

And then the dangerous part. The owner's SAVEGAME directory is where the entire export's starting state is read from. Our own Ctrl-S writes to that same directory. One keypress would have replaced the starting state with a played save, and the only thing that would have objected is a test asserting that none of 2048 flags is set. The view now copies the directory aside once before its first write and refuses to save if it cannot make the copy.

The explosion shielded nobody

Merging the creature and object id spaces was the second decision, and it got a class where the order has no default: a caller that does not decide which side comes first will not compile. That sounds pedantic. It found a defect the same day.

The explosion scan stops at the first candidate whose tile blocks missiles. The view fed it every actor first, so no object could ever come before an actor, and nothing on a square could shield anything. The comment said outright that the order was not measured, and it was honest when it was written. Measuring it turned the answer over.

It is still a divergence, and both the comment and the test say so: objects first shields every actor on a square holding one blocking object, where the original shields only whoever sits behind it in that square's own chain. Objects first keeps the behaviour in existence and over-applies it; actors first had removed it.

Nought degrees is northern

The sextant prints a latitude and a longitude, and four things in that arithmetic are easy to get wrong. The letter test is strictly greater than nought, so the eight rows south of the origin print nought north and nothing ever prints nought south. The divide is an arithmetic shift rather than a division, which separates one square north of the origin: the shift says one, the division says nought.

The third is a trap. Changing level has its own coordinate conversion that keeps a tile's place inside its chunk, and the print uses a plain shift. They differ by up to two degrees. The conversion looks like the right formula and is a different routine, so a test pins the difference to stop anyone substituting one for the other.

The dig's two rolls, and a band that separated nothing

Digging works almost nowhere: four tiles on levels 1 to 4, plus one hard-coded square on the surface. A fountain is one in 32 and a gold nugget one in ten of the digs that were not a fountain, so a nugget's real share is 31 in 320.

I proved that by breaking it: I swapped the two rolls, and all twelve tests passed. The swap leaves the "found nothing" share at exactly 279 in 320 and moves the other two by only a hundred in 32000. My bands were three hundred wide. Measured over 320000 digs: 9758, 31642, 278600. The order is now pinned by an exact forty-outcome run from one seed, and swapping them again fails it.

Three squares that cannot be reached, and they are the design

The orb of the moons opens a red gate where you point inside a five by five square, and the formula gives the gate a quality of 1 to 25. Three of those can never be produced, because the three central squares of the party's own row take a branch that means "stay where you are".

That looked like a bug. It is not: the destination tables are written as five rows of five, which is the orb's own grid, and at exactly those three indices the tables are nought. The formula and the table agree perfectly. The suspicion was withdrawn and nothing went into the bug list.

The horse, which nobody had listed

I asked the dispatch itself which usable types still answer "not built" rather than assembling a list by grep. Eleven. The plan's own account names thirteen types and mentions neither horse, nor the vortex cube, nor the gargish scroll.

A mounted horse is two objects, and the rear half is one step backward from the facing. The rider does not get a vehicle: the rider becomes a mounted horse, which is why dismounting needs the original shape back out of the save.

And the best part is a line that looks absurd. The original sets the horse's type to gold immediately before deleting it, and its own comment says why: so the deletion does not also take the rear half, which the mount is about to reuse. The brief demanded an answer before that line was copied. The answer was no: this project's actor deletion never walks a creature's extra tiles at all, so the retype would defeat a walk there is none of. Left out, with the reason recorded.

A book's text, and what hides it

A book now uses the same paging text window the conversation uses, and its text prints. But what came out of that is its own post, and it is the next one.
