Teleporting Is the Default

The previous post listed five missing things, and the first of them was described backwards in the docs. Teleporting is not a fallback for a character stranded outside the window. It is the first thing tried, and a search is what happens once teleporting refuses.

The direction was reversed

docs/u6-pathfinding.md claimed: a character outside the window, or whose goal is, gets teleported, unless it is within five squares of the player. That reads sensibly. The shape is still backwards: the original offers teleporting first, to every character looking for a path, before it knows anything about the window, and only the one teleporting refuses falls through to a search.

The difference is not cosmetic. At the Avatar's starting spot at noon, three characters stand within five squares of the player and one more has a goal within five, so four refuse and need a search. The other 32 teleport, three a turn. The old rule would have routed nine and teleported the rest: a different set, four characters different, and the difference runs one way. Everyone "near" is also inside the old window test, but four inside the window are not near.

The character's own level, never the goal's

Two guards, not one. First it asks whether the character itself is within five of the player. Teleporting continues, reads the character's schedule entry, and asks again whether that is within five. Both times on the level that belongs to the character, not to the goal.

The decompilation remarks on this itself, in its own comment. It is not an oversight, and I deliberately did not fold it into one "either side is near" test over both fields: a character standing on the player's level whose goal sits on another one behaves differently from what a combined test would suggest, and the difference only shows in the rare case that makes combining them tempting in the first place.

A route is now a state, not a queue

The old implementation unpacked the whole route into a queue of squares right after the search. That worked as long as nothing blocked the way: a blocked route was simply thrown away and a new one asked for.

The original does not throw anything away on the first try. It retries the same step for three turns (AI_ONPATH → AI_PATH_BLOCKED1 → AI_PATH_BLOCKED2 → AI_PATH_FAILED), and one successful step puts it straight back. A queue cannot be asked to retry an entry it has already handed out, so following a route is now its own per-slot state, a counter for the run it is on and the steps already taken in it, rather than a precomputed list.

A table that reads past its own end

On the third blocked turn the character gives up, and asks whether it ended up exactly next to its goal. The test indexes a direction table with the raw run byte, the direction combined with the steps left, rather than the direction alone.

The direction table is eight bytes. A run with two or three steps left (run byte 8 through 15) does not fit inside it: it reads the next table, then bytes of the pointer table after that, as if they were a direction. I measured that independently, two different ways, and they agreed on the same garbage: 

run byte     8    9   10   11   12   13   14   15
dx          -1   -1    0    1    1    1    0   -1
dy          38    2   44    2   54    2   59    2

This paragraph first said that a run with one step left (run byte 4 through 7) fits inside the table and therefore works correctly. It fits. It is not correct. The step indexes the table at (run byte & 3) * 2, so indices 0, 2, 4 and 6. The give-up test indexes with the whole byte. Those agree only at index 0, which marks the end of a route and never reaches this test.

So not one entry is the step the character was taking, and the four inside the table are worse than garbage: their offset stays within a square, so they can read as close enough from a square that is not beside the goal at all. Run byte 6 is one step south and reads the offset (-1, 0): a character stopped north of its goal does not settle, and one stopped east of it does.

The visible consequence: a character that gets blocked exactly beside its goal usually does not settle into its action but searches for a new route instead, and occasionally settles from the wrong square. I did not read run bytes sixteen and above: that would reach ever deeper into pointers and then into name strings, and the code refuses rather than guesses from there.

The error was mine and it travelled from the handover brief into the code's table, its comment, the bug log and this post. A test that claimed the same thing held it in place. All twelve entries are now pinned as measured values, with a discriminator on top that requires no entry to be the step being taken. Deriving them from our own code is exactly what produced the four wrong ones.

Drifting outside the window is its own mechanism

A separate thing from following a route: a character whose square falls outside the view's window mid-route is frozen. It is teleported with no three-a-turn limit, because scrolling the view away from under it must not simply strand it, and if that refuses too, the route slot is freed and it goes back to searching.
