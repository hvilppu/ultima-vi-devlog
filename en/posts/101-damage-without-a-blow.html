Damage without a blow

The previous slice found the real blocker to wiring the winds in: three of the four wind spells need a routine that takes hit points off an actor, and nothing in this project could do that except by resolving a blow against it. This was not a gap in measurement: the original's own damage routine had already been measured and built in full, behind one door that demanded a blow. The job was to open a second door into the same routine.

Engagement, provoking and severity belong to the attack, not the damage

The original's __DoHit does three things in order: it writes the engagement (Leader), it calls the damage routine LoseHP, and it gives the experience that routine returns to the attacker. Only after that, outside the routine, does the neutral-provoke check run and the wound severity show. The wind itself is the proof of this split: its energy case calls the damage routine and then, separately, adds the experience, shows the severity and provokes with a constant of one. If those were inside the routine, the case would not need to do them itself.

Not a copy: the same private halves

The new public entry takes an actor and an amount, not a blow and not an attacker, and its own body is short: the immortal class check, the already-dead check, and then exactly the same two methods the old blow-shaped entry already calls for an ordinary defender. Death, the class split and the blood pool all run through one code path behind two doors, not two separate answers to what a death does. The experience is returned now too, not granted: the old routine handed it straight to the attacker from inside, because an attacker was always available there; the new door has none, so the routine computes and returns it, and the caller decides who gets it.

Three winds, and a fourth that needed no door of its own

Three composed tests run the wind's own handler with the real damage routine and real character data: flame deals the right damage, energy kills and returns the right experience, death kills outright. The death wind raised an open question: does it need its own separate kill routine? Structurally, no: the kill spell's own handler, already built earlier, calls the same damage routine with the victim's own current hit points as the amount, and the new door kills outright whenever the hit points are at or below that amount. Whether this actually matches what the original's own kill routine does was not traced, here or before.

What this opened, and what it did not

The wind's own wiring into the view is no closer to running in the game: four other inputs, each reading a target's own class flags, are still unbuilt. But any spell that needs nothing more than "take hit points off an actor" can now call that directly; it is no longer an impossible request.
