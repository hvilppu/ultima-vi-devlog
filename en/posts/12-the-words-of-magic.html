Ultima VI rebuilt

Notes from a 1:1 reimplementation in C# and Unity.

← all posts · 12

The words of magic

The spell data is in no file at all. It is compiled into the program, and what gave it away was a feature that started life as nothing but headroom for spells that were never added.

The creature statistics had to be dug out of the executable earlier, because they appear in no data file. The spells live in the same place. The difference is that this time the search turned out to be unusually safe, and the reason is a pleasing one.

The gaps give the table away

Ultima VI has eight circles of magic with ten spells each, so eighty spells. But the numbering reserves sixteen numbers per circle. Six numbers in every circle are empty, most likely headroom that was never used.

The ingredient table is 128 bytes, one per spell number. So 48 of them are always zero, and those zeros sit in exact places: the last six of every group of sixteen. 

circle 1  61 82 82 60 24 12 50 00 84 80  00 00 00 00 00 00
circle 2  82 84 0E 1C 16 0D 12 88 88 2C  00 00 00 00 00 00
circle 3  A2 84 84 81 A8 60 52 03 E0 A0  00 00 00 00 00 00
...                                      ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
                                         six zeros, always

I searched the whole 200 kilobyte program for places where this pattern repeats eight times and the other slots are mostly non-zero. There was exactly one hit. The shape needed no extra condition at all, which is a stronger result than the creature table, where I needed four conditions together.

The names were at the end of pointers

The spell names are not in the table but stored as pointers into the program's data segment. And where that segment starts is recorded nowhere, so a pointer value of 0x135F says nothing on its own.

I could have searched for some known string and taken the difference, but that would have leaned on whatever happens to be in the game's text. A better way was in the pointers themselves: when consecutive pointers address consecutive strings, the gaps between them are the lengths of those strings. 

pointers   135F 136D 1378 1384 138F 139B 13A2 13AA
gaps         14   11   12   11   12    7    8

So I looked for the base address at which all eight pointers land on printable text and all seven gaps match the string lengths. There is exactly one such base in the whole file. The search leans on no word of English, only on proportions of length.

After that everything else opened up, because the tables sit next to each other in the data segment: the names, the 26 syllables, the incantation letters, the ingredient names and the circle names were all at fixed distances from the ingredient table.

Letters are syllables

The incantations surprised me in how compactly they are stored. Each spell has a run of two to four letters, and each letter indexes a table of 26 syllables, where A is the first and Z the last. 

IL    -> In Lor               Light
IM    -> In Mani              Heal
IMC   -> In Mani Corp         Resurrect
VCBM  -> Vas Corp Bet Mani    Armageddon

The syllables are read from the program rather than written into the code. They are data, and a reimplementation has no business deciding what the words of magic are.

These four are also in the tests, for a reason: their incantations are published in the game's own documentation, so they confirm the extraction from outside the data rather than against itself. Most tests compare data to itself, which is why the few external anchors are worth having.

The bit order is not what you would expect

The ingredient byte is a bit mask over eight substances. Those substances are object types 65 to 72, consecutively. But the bit order does not follow the object type order: 

bit 0  0x01  mandrake root   type 69
bit 1  0x02  nightshade      type 70
bit 2  0x04  black pearl     type 65
bit 3  0x08  blood moss      type 66
bit 4  0x10  spider silk     type 71
bit 5  0x20  garlic          type 67
bit 6  0x40  ginseng         type 68
bit 7  0x80  sulfurous ash   type 72

This is exactly the kind of place that would go wrong quietly: the wrong order would swap ingredients between spells and nothing would crash. So the object types of the bits are cross checked against my own LOOK.LZD, matching a number read from the program against a name in a data file.

A couple of details from the data

The most demanding spell in ingredients is Resurrect, needing six of the eight. The most used substance is sulfurous ash at 43 spells, the rarest is ginseng at 13. Two spells need no ingredients at all, and one of them is Armageddon: the most expensive spell in the eighth circle in magic points, consuming nothing but the caster.

What did not exist

The plan promised to extract each spell's effect and its targeting type as well. That was a wrong expectation: they are not a table. Each spell has its own branch of code that decides for itself whether it asks for a direction, a square, a character, or nothing.

I recorded that in the plan as an expectation that turned out wrong, rather than quietly dropping the item. The spells get implemented one at a time in phase C, and each will need its own test then.
