The ground began to hurt

Stepping onto a fire field did nothing. The get command printed a bracketed sentence where the original takes hit points off, and walking did not fire the terrain at all. Both got built, and on the way a defect turned up that no test could see: standing in lava could not kill anybody.

Seven arms and two fall-throughs

Terrain damage is one decision with seven arms: web, swamp, poison field, fire, tangle vine, force field and sleep field. Two of them fall through into the next, and that is the whole plot of the structure: a swamp without equipped boots is a poison field, and a tangle vine that is not itself a tangle vine is a force field. Eight independent arms would be wrong twice.

The hurt-this-turn bit is set first and unconditionally, before the dead test and before the tile is even looked at. That reads like a mistake and is not: the bit means something has already happened to this creature this turn, so the ground does not add its own. A creature standing on a tile no arm recognises therefore comes away with the bit and no damage, and that has a test of its own.

One line read correctly, its routine not read at all

The brief read one line correctly and its routine not at all, and that is what decided the design. The line looked like a plain clear of the bit after a move. Three lines are a gate, a clear and an immediate apply: inside the move, right after the new position is written. So the terrain pass has two callers rather than one, and the second is exactly what makes stepping onto a fire field hurt during the step instead of at the next minute. The clear is there so the new tile can fire.

There are two gates. Actors only, not map objects. And a global flag whose twenty hits include thirteen save-and-restore brackets around relocations, so it means "this move is a real step, not a relocation". A teleport, a level change, a moongate and a party regroup therefore fire no terrain. This was built as a parameter rather than a global flag, defaulting off, so all eight pre-existing call sites keep leaving the bit alone.

The comment that hid the defect

The view's terrain wiring clamped hit points at nought by hand, and its own remarks said the death branch was not built here. Half of that was true: the hit path needs an attacker, and the ground is not an attacker. But it has an attacker-less sibling that takes only a victim and an amount, handles Lord British's immortality itself, and reaches the survival or death branch. And the same view already called it five thousand lines further down.

So standing in lava could not kill anybody, and an absence claim in a comment is what kept that invisible. It now kills through the same corpse, karma and party handling a combat death takes. The defect was found by following our own comment rather than believing it, and that grew into a measurement of its own, told separately.

Eleven tiles, not ten, and one gate the brief missed

The build run corrected the brief twice and was right both times. The fire arm is eleven tiles rather than ten: lava and three around it, the campfire, four cook fire tiles, the fire field, and a bookstand that the decompilation's own comment marks with a question mark and that is in the arm regardless. The fire field rolls 1d30 and the other ten roll 1d8, either doubled for a fire-vulnerable class, and the doubling is asserted against the same roll rather than against a second draw.

And the terrain pass exempts flying creatures entirely, which the brief had missed. Ten of the sixty-four classes in the owner's own exported table carry the flying trait. The same run first wrote that the trait does not exist, which was the third false absence claim of the same night; the trait's own summary says in as many words that ground effects do not touch a flier.

And one window that is not there

The second correction was worse and it was mine. The brief said the routine masks the position against a forty by forty window around the map centre, and listed that arithmetic as unread. There is no such window. The line is a level comparison: nothing happens for an actor on a level other than the one on screen, and what follows it is the world's own coordinate wrap at 1024 and 256. So I described a rule I had not read, and the run caught it by doing exactly what the brief told it to do: reading the routine.
