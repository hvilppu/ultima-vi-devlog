A clock with a reason to tick

M14's slice E1: the town's clock moved off the fixed five-minute step and started running on the turn scheduler the previous slice built but never wired to anything. Walking is now the first thing that actually uses it, and wiring it up turned up two places that would have granted movement points twice and one place that would have frozen the player's own turn for good.

Two grants, and both had to stop

Before this slice, U6DailyRound.Apply granted DEXTE to every character from 2 to 223 that had a shape, and only afterward came the filter that skips anyone not on their routine or backing out of a fight. The grant happened before the filter, not instead of it, which means a character fixed in combat was granted exactly the same as one following its schedule. Nobody noticed, because nothing until now read those points at minute granularity. U6View.RunDailyRound did the same for the Avatar separately, because the Avatar falls outside the range Apply governs and never got a grant from it at all.

Now that U6TurnScheduler.GrantRound grants every minute, either of the old grants would have produced the same DEXTE twice every game hour: once from the scheduler, once from code that has no idea the scheduler exists. Both were removed, and a test builds exactly this situation directly: a character is forced into combat, its points are granted once by the scheduler, and then the hour that used to grant a second time is run against it. The points land exactly where one grant would leave them.

A clock that only ticks when there is a reason

The old clock walked five minutes forward on every step, regardless of what the character did or how nimble it was. U6TurnScheduler.Advance does the opposite: it looks for the next actor with the largest share of its movement points, and the clock only ticks a minute when nobody at all is eligible. The player's own step still spends its own cost exactly as before, and after that the view loops the scheduler until the turn comes back to the Avatar itself, exactly where the original stops too.

A discriminator was already sitting there waiting: the town's own schedules send a batch of characters walking at noon, and measured by distance alone, not one of them would have missed the hour at the original's own pace, where the old fixed step missed on sixteen of twenty-three. What the live game mechanics actually reproduce is a smaller but tighter set: of the characters that refuse an immediate teleport because they or their goal are too close to the player, not one misses the hour at the new pace either. That set is four, not twenty-three, because most distant characters simply teleport into place and never walk a single step.

The Avatar that left its own level and never came back

The scheduler's own eligibility test reads a character's level straight out of U6CharacterData. The Avatar's own row there had never needed to stay current, because everything else in the view, party following, the character layer, finding the nearest conversation partner, already read its position from the view's own spatial index instead. The two had drifted apart since the day that index was built, and neither side noticed.

The scheduler is the first reader that asks the raw table for a character's level directly, and without a fix the very first ladder down would have been enough: the Avatar's row would stay on the old level, the scheduler would never consider it eligible on the new one, and the player's own turn would never come back. The fix is one method, called once at the top of the loop before it asks the scheduler anything, and the same pass closed two other spots, two different ways of using a ladder, that had not even been keeping the spatial index current.

A finding left open: the vehicle's own clock

Travelling by vehicle does not charge the Avatar's own points, because that is its own slice of work, left for later on purpose. Under the old fixed step that was a harmless detail, because the clock advanced regardless. Under the new one it is not: since the clock only ticks when nobody at all is eligible, and the Avatar is always eligible with a full share when nothing ever spends it, a lone vehicle trip does not move the clock at all. That is a real regression from the fixed step it replaces, not a simplification chosen on purpose, and it only closes once the vehicle's own points pool is wired in.
