Six things nothing could reach

The use command answered "not built yet" for twenty-two types. Six of those had their rule built and tested in this project, and one group of them was already reachable from a different key. The measurement was the whole finding, and it was taken before anything was written.

Eighty-five types, and a count that counts itself

The original's usable list is exactly 85 entries, nine rows, no duplicates, and the loop that reads it reads exactly 85. This project already matched that number and asserted it in a test of its own. Running all 85 through our own dispatch, the rune range included: 63 reached a named handler and 22 answered "not built yet".

The handler side was not the thin part. It has an arm for all 26 handlers, so every handler that exists is reachable. The gap was entirely in which type maps to which handler.

And the 22 were not one thing. Four vehicles, whose boarding and leaving is built, tested and reachable from the Enter key. A powder keg, complete except for one lock. A torch, half built. Two horses, not built and not vehicle types on this side either. And fifteen genuinely unbuilt, named rather than counted.

Now 69 of 85, and the count is pinned by a test that runs every one of the 85 through the dispatch and counts them. The next slice's progress will therefore be a number rather than a claim.

The vehicle arm is one guard and a 43-line dispatcher

The brief first said the arm is "one guard and one call". The guard is one line and the call is a 43-line dispatcher that goes both ways: if you are aboard this very vehicle it tries to leave, otherwise to board, with four refusals in between. A deed check for a ship or skiff whose quality is not nought. An inflated balloon becoming a deflated one when you leave it. At sea, leaving a ship onto the skiff it was carrying, and boarding a ship from a skiff stowing the skiff aboard.

The conclusion survived anyway, because every one of those six branches is already built and the Enter key calls the same dispatcher in the same shape. So the use arm really does add only its guard. With one exception: resolving a multi-tile vehicle to its head object needs a real search of a square's objects, and the look command's own search delegate is deliberately empty, so the use arm needed its own.

The keg's lock was the slice's only new rule

The original has one burning fuse in the whole world, as one counter and one object reference. A second keg refuses until the first one's three minutes are up.

The second half of the discriminator is the half that matters, because a latch passes the first: a second keg refuses while the fuse burns and lights once the first has gone out. The run proved it by breaking it, making the clock's own step a no-op, and failed on exactly the assertion that says the lock clears.

Readying a torch is not an unconditional step

The brief presented the torch's fourth step as unconditional: ready it in a free hand. The run read the routine and corrected it. The step is wrapped in a test for not being on the map, and then again in a test for being in a pack or a container. So a torch split off an on-map pile stays on the map and lights in place; only one that came from a pack or container ever reaches a hand.

And three numbers from the same lines that the brief did not have: a lit torch gets frame one, a quantity of 200 which is its remaining burning time, and a clock refresh so the light updates. The 200 is named as unbuilt with its citation, because nothing burns a torch down yet and inventing the value without a consumer would be guessing rather than building. The rule that consumes the lit flag is built: the count printer prints the article rather than a count for a lit torch, because its quantity is not a count.

The leaked-text check earned its keep

The run had quoted the original's own text rows verbatim into test comments. The check caught nine findings, and they were rewritten as an offset and a description in this project's own convention. The re-run was clean. This is the check that compares every string literal in the whole repository against the decompilation's own literals rather than one session's diff, and it has now caught both a build run's comments and one of my own briefs.
