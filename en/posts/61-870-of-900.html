M14's casting gateway is measured and built: what happens between choosing a line in the spellbook and the spell doing anything. Three refusals in order, two costs, taking the reagents, and three resistance rolls that are all the same one line with different figures.

A halving that changes everything

Every resistable spell rolls ((target / 2) + 30 - caster) / 2 > 1d30, all integer division. The target's figure is halved and the caster's is not. It looks like the same idea as the attack roll, which halves neither, and that resemblance is exactly the trap: the two do not share a line of code in this project, and they should not.

The measurement ran every pair of target and caster figures from 1 to 30, 900 pairs in all. The halved and unhalved readings differ in threshold in 870 of them and observably in 867: three pairs have thresholds of 0 and 1, which against a die of 1 to 30 both mean never resisting, so their difference cannot be seen in play. The smaller number is the one that holds, which is why it is here. The unhalved reading is never the lower of the two, so a port that forgets the halving makes every resistable spell weaker across the board, not just sometimes. Two evenly matched characters at 30 resist 20.0% of the time with the halved formula and 46.7% with the unhalved one, more than twice the difference, in exactly the situation that is most ordinary: two characters of equal strength facing each other.

Three refusals, one message at a time

Casting from the book asks three things in this order: level below the circle, magic points below the circle, the spellbook page's own castable count at zero. Only the first one that fires is reported, so a caster short on all three only ever hears about the level. Only the third consults the reagents, and it does so through a count the page itself already computed rather than recounting the inventory: the reagent check is a question about display state, not about the items themselves.

A fourth inventory walk

This project has now measured four different ways of walking the object list, and they are not interchangeable: one walks recursively and stops at the end of the owner's own chain, another stops at the first item the owner does not own, a third stops at the first thing lying on the map, and the reagent take's own search does not stop at anything at all. That last one can therefore walk past the caster's own belongings and find something somebody else owns somewhere else on the map. It costs nothing, because the search only decides whether to attempt a take, and the take itself is always scoped to the caster: a stray match never moves anyone else's reagent onto the caster's side of the ledger.

A write that does not say who it hits

The path a mouse-clicked spell target takes writes the reagent counter outside its own eight-byte table, indexed by the target's object number rather than by a line number. The measurement is exact about that much: the write is real and its index is measured. What it does not claim is which byte it actually hits, because the decompilation's own memory addresses were measured, in an earlier slice, to be ten bytes off from the owner's own build. Naming the victim would be a guess dressed up as a measurement, so it stays unnamed: recorded in originalBugs.md as an anomaly with that limitation stated, not reproduced, because there is nothing in this project at that address to corrupt.
