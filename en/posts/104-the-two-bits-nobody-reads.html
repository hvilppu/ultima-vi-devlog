The two bits nobody reads

Curse and protection are two of the missile switch's last four cases, and each is a one line effect in the original: set a bit, nothing else. Wiring them alone would have been worthless, because everything that gives those two bits a meaning was already built in this project and hardcoded off in five places, six once the count was measured again.

Four cases, one of them can fail

Curse and protection set a bit and do nothing else: no contest, no message, no halo. Paralyze sets a different bit and shows a hit, but has no immunity check at all, the only one of this switch's status spells that always lands. Invisibility alone can fail: its own gate was already measured and built in another slice, so this case was just a call into its own refusal.

The other half mattered more than the fourth case

The curse and protection formulas already existed: minus three from strength, dexterity and intelligence when cursed, floored at one; plus three to armour when protected. They just had no real caller. The attack path passed a literal "not cursed" in six places and read the armour past the protection formula entirely. Both fixed: the character table can now be asked whether it is cursed or protected, and the attack reads the answer instead of assuming it.

One bit, three names

The curse bit is the same physical bit as the egg's own "hatched" and the embryo's own "mutant", three names for one bit in the original's own header. Not a live collision in this project: the mutant flag's only reader always asks about an embryo's own number inside an egg, and the curse flag's six readers never ask about an egg at all. Tested both directions, not trusted from the name.

A cursed creature is worse at shaking off its own curse

The per minute rolls that wear these statuses off read intelligence as the curse has already lowered it, not the raw figure. That means a cursed creature's own test to shake off its curse uses exactly the intelligence the curse itself reduced: a real feedback loop in the original, and it falls out for free from using the right formula rather than from a separate rule.
