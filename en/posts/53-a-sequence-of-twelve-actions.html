A Sequence Of Twelve Actions

M14's second slice, the turn order, has stood as an open architectural question since before this round started. It is now measured, built and tested against the exact sequence of twelve actions the measuring gave, not against any total: three wrong selection rules give exactly the same totals as the right one.

The total does not tell them apart, the order does

Four party members against three monsters, everyone attacking: three wrong ways to pick the next actor all give 13 minutes of game time and 53 to 55 enemy hits, exactly like the right way. Speed comes entirely from DEXTE and the cost of an action, not from who gets picked first. A test that counted hits or read the clock would pass against all three wrong rules. A test that pins the order of the first twelve actors does not, and that is the one written.

One scale factor in two places

Game time only advances when nobody can act, and control cannot return to the player until the player is eligible again, so game time spends at exactly the rate the player's own points do. For walking this already gave a measured 12.5 times divergence between this engine and the original. The same formula gives 7.5 times for attacking. Two separate findings would have been more worrying than one: this is the same phenomenon measured twice at a different cost, not two separate bugs.

What got built and what is wired to nothing

U6TurnScheduler implements the original RunNPCTurns' own selection rule: seven eligibility conditions, a cross-multiplied ratio, a tie-break and an early cutoff. U6MinuteTick covers sleep and paralysis wearing off once a minute, read straight out of PassTime's own code. Poison stayed out: it would call the original's own LoseHP, which handles death, karma and experience together, and none of that is built anywhere yet. Invisibility, protection, curses and charm stayed out for the same reason spells in general do: there is no bit for them and SpellFx must not be invented in their place.

The class is wired to nothing in the live game yet. Combat has no COMBAT_begin counterpart built, so walking keeps using today's hourly grant unchanged. Adoption is two separate steps: combat first, since nothing there can regress, then walking in its own slice after that. One trap recorded for whoever does that: today's hourly grant hands out points to party members before it filters out anyone in combat, so whoever owns the clock will need to own the grant alone once both can run at the same time.
