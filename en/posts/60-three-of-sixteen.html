Three of Sixteen

Every combat mode in M14 is now measured. Slice C3b took the five that were left: fleeing in four flavours, two ways of feeling about the party, an immobile monster, and a tangle vine pod whose own random growth cancels itself a quarter of the time it tries.

Four fleeing modes, one handler

AI_RETREAT, AI_SHY, AI_FEAR and AI_SCHED_SHY share one function and differ in three places: how close a threat has to get before they flee (8 tiles or 3), whether they fight back when a flight fails, and whether they walk home to the party once they are out of flee range. Only two of them fight back, only the good ones walk home, and the step is the actor's own square mirrored away from the threat, not a single tile toward it. Beyond seven tiles from the Avatar the handler does not even try: movement points are set to zero outright, a different kind of stop from a wait, visible as a difference in who moves next. And only AI_RETREAT ever recovers: one time in four it gains a hit point, and only then does it check whether that still leaves it critical; a reading above zero hands the mode back to whatever combat stance was stored, in the very same roll that healed it.

The flip that cancels itself

The tangle vine pod grows toward its target, or, with nothing better to do, in a random cardinal direction one time in four. That random direction gets flipped 180 degrees whenever it would otherwise match the pod's own current heading. The reasoning is sound enough: a pod should not grow straight back into its own body. But the growth routine itself already refuses exactly the one direction that is 180 degrees from the pod's own heading, before it spends anything. So the flip never steers growth away from the forbidden direction. It steers it straight into it. 

a turn attempts a random growth           0.2500
of those, cancelled by the flip           0.2500
a turn actually grows randomly            0.1875  (3/16)
a port that dropped the flip would grow   0.2500  (4/16)

Three growth attempts in sixteen actually succeed, not four, and the attempt itself costs nothing, because the refusal happens before a single step is paid for. The number did not come from the arithmetic alone: an engine test runs the pod's own final branch sixteen thousand times over one seeded generator and counts how often the straight move is actually called, not merely how often the attempt begins. Nobody has reported seeing this in play; it waits in originalBugs.md with a mechanism attached, not a fix.

A frame table read flat, not two columns wide

When a growth succeeds, the pod picks itself a new frame from a table the original wrote as eight rows of two columns. The pod's own read indexes the row by direction and the column by a four-sided die, which reaches column three of a table that is nominally two columns wide. The largest index either read reaches is two times six plus three, which is fifteen: the table's own last byte, neither past its end nor safely inside its own row. A port that bounded the column to the row would only change how the pod looks, and no existing test would notice; this project's own test builds the table out of distinctive values and shows directly that index fifteen was the one actually read.

A conversion that does not revert

AI_UNFRIENDLY is hostile but holds its fire unless the party comes within three tiles. Then, one time in eight, it writes its own mode to AI_ASSAULT straight into its own stored field, not just this turn's choice, and attacks at once. Nothing in this project ever writes it back: a creature that rolls that one in eight is hostile for the rest of the fight, and the rest of the game.
