The same formula at two addresses

Looking into a crystal ball takes an intelligence check, and failing it hurts. The check is rand(1,30) < (45 - intelligence) / 2. A lockpick's breaking check is rand(1,30) < (45 - dexterity) / 2. In the original these are two separate routines at two addresses, byte for byte the same body. Here there is one, and a test runs both over every ability value and asserts they agree. 83 of the 85 usable types now reach their own handler.

The threshold truncates, and 42 is where the risk ends

Dividing by two truncates, so abilities 10 and 11 share a threshold of 17, and 12 and 13 share 16. The interesting end is the top: from 42 upward the threshold is 1 and the lowest roll is 1, so the check cannot fail at all. The draw is spent anyway, because the original rolls before it compares, and that is the kind of thing one shared random stream makes measurable: short-circuiting it would make every later roll in the session a different roll.

South is positive, and a number with no letter is the same as a junk letter

The ball asks for a latitude and a longitude in degrees. The parse reads digits and the first non-digit decides everything: S and E are positive, N and W negative, so 40N is -40. Anything after the letter is never looked at.

And the likeliest typing mistake, a bare number with no letter, answers 100, exactly as a wrong letter does. Both bounds reject 100, so the refusal arrives as a value rather than as an error, and the rule never tests for a missing letter at all. It is one of those places where the original's shape is tidier than anything I would have written.

Four bounds, three of which are explained

latitude    refused above 80 or below -44
longitude   refused above 88 or below -37

accepted range on the map:  rows 8-1000, columns 8-1008
one degree past the lower:  row 0, column 0
one degree past the upper:  row 1008, column 1016  (still on the map)

I wrote into the class's remarks that all four bounds keep the eye on the map. The arithmetic refuted it: the lower bounds are exactly one degree above the value that would land on nought, but the upper ones have slack. Three of the four numbers are accounted for and one pair is not, and it is recorded that way rather than tidied into a story.

And the sound flag, which moves no dice

The original switches the sound off around the eye and restores it afterwards. I wrote that this changes how many values the ball's eye draws, because this project has already measured that the sound handler's early return above every drawing arm does exactly that elsewhere. I checked, and it does not do it here: the eye's only sound is its own cast effect, which sits inside if(isSpell) and is skipped when the ball calls it as an object, and nothing in its loop plays anything. The difference between the ball's eye and the spell's is real, but it comes from that flag and not from the sound.

The switch is still recorded as a constant, because whoever next measures the eye's own consumption will want to know it exists. What it is not is read, and the field that had been left unread in the view came out in the same pass.

Two structural changes instead of a copy

The damage-and-severity pair sat in the view as ApplyTerrainDamageHit, named after whoever called it first. What it carries is the original's own LoseHP followed by COMBAT_displaySeverity, nothing terrain-specific, and the crystal ball is its second caller. The name moved rather than the code being copied. The wizard eye's opening is likewise one method now, shared by the spell and the ball.
