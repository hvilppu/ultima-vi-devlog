Four Vehicles and One of Our Own Bugs

A ship, a skiff, a raft and an inflated balloon all sail in the engine now. Measuring and building fit in the same round this time, because the measurement was unusually thorough: boarding, unboarding, four terrain rules and the restrictions of being aboard were all written down before a single line of C# existed.

Boarding is a command, not a step

You cannot board by walking onto a vehicle; it is a "use" command, shared in the original by the ship, the skiff, the raft and the inflated balloon. That same command is also what refuses: in solo mode nobody can board, a ship or a skiff needs the right deed, and the distance has to be within one square in either direction. Leaving is its own chapter, because a ship tests only two directions when leaving, perpendicular to its own hull, while everything else tests all eight, and because CanLeaveVehicleAt's result is never the landing square: the party always lands on the vehicle's own square, because the ship's hull, the skiff and the raft are marked "bridge" for a walker, a tile that admits a walker regardless of what the base tile underneath says.

Four terrain classes, not one

CanMoveTo splits movers into four classes. A walker asks "not impassable". A ship asks "impassable and wet", deep water, and cannot reach the shallow even though it is water. A skiff or a raft asks the same or just "wet", so they can. A balloon asks "not a wall", which is a big difference from "not impassable": the wrong reading would give exactly the walker's own number, and the whole vehicle would be pointless. Measured: 1012237 admissible surface squares for the balloon, the wrong reading: 295751, exactly the walker's own.

Our own bug, found on the way

While counting the four classes, the walker's own number did not match the measurement's own number, and the cause turned out to be this engine's own mistake: U6Collision rejected wet terrain for a walker outright, not just impassable terrain. The original's rule is "not impassable", full stop, and the shallow (30403 surface squares) is wet but passable. A walker could have waded in shallow water the whole time in the original and could not here, and nobody noticed because no earlier test had asked exactly this question. Fixed in the same change, with its own test pinning the number: 30397 admissible shallow squares, six fewer than 30403, because six of them carry an impassable object of their own.

The representation aboard is our own, the behaviour is not

The original clones the leader onto object 0 and writes the vehicle's own shape onto it, keeping the real vehicle object alive inside object 0's own inventory. This engine's object world does not allow changing an object's type in place, and does not allow putting a character inside one, so U6Vehicles does it differently: boarding destroys the real map object and remembers its type, quality, hull strength and frame; unboarding recreates a fresh object with the same numbers. Everything observable, what is on the map, where, and who can do what, comes out the same either way.

The mounted horse stayed unbuilt

The owner chose to build everything except the mounted horse, and the reason is measured: neither of the game's two shipped horses can be taken through the normal "use" command, because both are actors below the number the command requires. The only route is a rare conversation opcode in three NPC scripts. The same shape as the furniture search found earlier: the code exists, the way to reach it does not.
